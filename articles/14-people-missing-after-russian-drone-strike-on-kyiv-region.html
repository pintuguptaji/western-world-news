14 people missing after Russian drone strike on Kyiv region

Timur Tkachenko, the head of the Kyiv Regional Military Administration, said the body of one person had been recovered from the rubble and that three people had been injured.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 22, 2026

At least 14 people were missing after a Russian drone destroyed part of a five-storey apartment building in Dymer, in Ukraine's Kyiv region, early on Sunday, November 22. One body had been recovered from the rubble and three people had been injured, Timur Tkachenko, the head of the Kyiv Regional Military Administration, said.

The drone struck the residential building in the town of Dymer, about 45 to 50 kilometres north of Kyiv in the Vyshhorod district, in the overnight hours. Part of the building collapsed from the ground floor through the fifth, and 25 apartments were damaged. Rescue teams spent the day picking through collapsed floors and twisted steel, searching for residents feared trapped beneath the rubble.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed the attack on X, saying at least 14 people may still be trapped. "Part of a five-story apartment building has collapsed following a Russian drone attack. I thank our first responders for acting quickly: nine people have already been evacuated, and the fire has been contained," he wrote. The nine evacuated residents included a child, and the fire at the site was brought under control.

The strike came during a night of heavy Russian drone and missile attacks across Ukraine. Ukraine's air force said Russia launched 186 drones overnight, including 72 jet-powered drones, and that it had neutralised 161 of them. In a separate attack, a Russian double-tap strike on Kryvyi Rih, using a ballistic missile followed by jet-powered drones, killed two people, including a seven-year-old, and wounded around 60, according to local officials.

Zelenskyy described the preceding seven days as one of the war's most intense aerial stretches, with Russian forces firing thousands of drones, guided bombs and missiles across 20 Ukrainian regions. The campaign has put severe strain on power grids and emergency services as winter approaches, and Ukraine has been pressing allied governments to speed up the delivery of anti-ballistic interceptors, as Moscow continues to use North Korean-supplied missiles in its air campaign.

The strike also sharpened Kyiv's argument for more air defenses. Zelenskyy used the attack to urge the United States to approve Patriot interceptors bought through NATO's PURL program, which lets European allies fund American weapons for Ukraine. Ukrainian officials say the week-long campaign is straining power grids and emergency services ahead of winter, and that Moscow has been using North Korean-supplied missiles in its air campaign. The appeal is a familiar one, but the Dymer strike gave it fresh urgency: the building that collapsed was not a military target, and the people under its rubble were civilians whose safety depended on interceptors that had not yet arrived.

As rescue teams pushed deeper into the collapsed section of the Dymer building, the figures were revised. Later reports put the number of missing at 15 and the death toll at two, with four people injured. Authorities cautioned that they could not confirm that all of the missing residents had been inside the building when it collapsed.

The Dymer strike closed out a brutal week for Ukraine's cities. A missile and drone attack on Kyiv's Darnytskyi district the same morning killed 22 people, with search and rescue operations continuing there as well. The pattern of strikes on residential areas, far from the front line, has become a regular feature of Russia's campaign, and Ukrainian officials say the aim is to wear down the population and the country's infrastructure before winter.

Search and rescue operations in Dymer remained under way as authorities worked to establish the fate of the missing residents. For the families waiting at the site, the hours after the strike offered little certainty, only the knowledge that nine of their neighbours had been pulled out alive and that the search would continue as long as there was any chance of finding more survivors.
