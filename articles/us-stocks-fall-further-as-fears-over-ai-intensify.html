US stocks fall further as fears over AI intensify

All three main US indexes were down in early trading, with the tech-dominated Nasdaq down almost 4%.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 25, 2026

US stocks fell further on Wednesday as fears over artificial intelligence intensified, with the tech-dominated Nasdaq plunging almost 4 per cent in early trading. All three main US indexes were down in early trading, and the sell-off capped a brutal stretch for Wall Street that has wiped trillions of dollars from the value of technology companies.

The Nasdaq Composite fell as much as 4.6 per cent shortly after the opening bell, before paring significant losses to close lower by 0.62 per cent. It was the index's second day of falls in a row, resuming declines after a rebound on Monday. After falling 1,100 points after the opening bell, the Dow erased those losses and gained 165 points, or 0.33 per cent. The S&P 500 closed lower by just 0.25 per cent, paring earlier losses, buoyed by gains in non-tech sectors. The Nasdaq 100, an index including the largest tech companies, briefly fell 20 per cent from its peak in early October, flirting with bear market territory, before paring losses.

The falls extended one of the worst weeks Wall Street has seen in years. On Friday November 20, the S&P 500 dropped 3.7 per cent to 6,910.39, the Nasdaq Composite plummeted 5.28 per cent to 22,617.73, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 949.73 points, or 1.93 per cent, to 48,147.33. It was Wall Street's worst week since the tariff turmoil of April 2025, and one of its worst since March 2020, when the Covid-19 pandemic triggered a global lockdown. The S&P 500's losing streak reached seven days, the longest since February 2020, and the Nasdaq had erased nearly 18 per cent since its all-time high on October 6, turning negative for 2026. Companies on the Nasdaq Composite lost more than 4 trillion dollars in market value in the course of the week.

Chipmakers and AI infrastructure stocks led the declines. Nvidia fell 8.7 per cent on Wednesday, and the broader Philadelphia Semiconductor Index lost 7.3 per cent. On Friday, Nvidia had sunk 14 per cent, its longest losing streak since the 2008 financial crisis, and the stock has now fallen roughly 48 per cent since its early October peak, wiping out more than 2.8 trillion dollars in market capitalisation in 45 days, more than the entire CAC 40 index. Advanced Micro Devices fell 13.3 per cent on Friday. Among other megacaps on Wednesday, Alphabet fell 5 per cent, Amazon shed 5.4 per cent, while Microsoft and Apple were down 3 per cent and 1.7 per cent respectively. Memory-chipmakers Sandisk and Micron Technology fell 5.6 per cent and 4.6 per cent. The S&P 500 technology sector led the losses with a 5.2 per cent fall.

Anthropic, the AI company that listed on the stock market only last week, has been caught in the storm. Its shares tumbled 9.1 per cent on Friday but managed to remain above its 83 dollar IPO price, and on Wednesday it dropped almost 10 per cent before paring losses and ending the day up by just over 1 per cent, an outlier among AI stocks. Bloom Energy, another AI infrastructure stock, sank 15.3 per cent on Friday and is now down more than 58 per cent from its June peak.

Traders have for weeks been fretting about the AI boom and whether the vast amounts of money being spent on its buildout will pay off for companies down the line. Investors are still nervous, and none of the fundamental questions about AI's return on investment from the heavy capital spending have been resolved, even with the unusual return of Jensen Huang as chief executive of Nvidia. Nvidia's strong earnings and the initial enthusiasm for Anthropic's IPO failed to soothe investor jitters this week.

Politics has added to the pressure. The Democrats' sweeping victory in the midterm elections has heightened fears about growing opposition to AI data center construction and increased regulatory scrutiny of the AI sector. Fresh data showing the US economy is still running hot has prompted traders to ramp up bets on interest rate hikes, alongside new tariff proposals from President Donald Trump that have added to geopolitical uncertainty. Higher interest rates could make the debt-funded AI buildout more expensive, dimming the outlook for AI-related stocks. Investors are just selling tech and fleeing for safety, said Jed Ellerbroek, portfolio manager at Argent Capital Management.

As AI-related stocks fell, investors rushed into bonds and scooped up shares in other areas of the stock market, including materials, real estate and energy. US Treasury yields were sharply lower on Wednesday morning as investors nervous about the AI trade rushed into bonds. Stocks across Asia and Europe were also mostly in the red as anxiety about AI rippled through global markets. The tech-heavy Nasdaq and the S&P 500 remained on track for their worst November since 2008, and the Nasdaq Composite recorded 424 new lows against only 17 new highs on Wednesday.

US markets will be closed for Thanksgiving on Thursday and operate a shortened session on Friday, closing early at 1 p.m. ET. When trading resumes, investors will be watching the next Federal Reserve meeting, which could result in higher interest rates to combat persistent inflation, and looking for any evidence that the massive spending on AI can deliver the profit and revenue goals the market expects. Without that evidence, there is little incentive for a rebound.
