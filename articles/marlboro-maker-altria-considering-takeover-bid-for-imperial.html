Marlboro maker Altria considering takeover bid for Imperial Brands

Shares in UK tobacco company jump by more than 14% on news US rival is looking into making offer. Business live, latest updates.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 26, 2026

The maker of Marlboro cigarettes is considering a takeover of its British rival Imperial Brands, in a deal that could reshape the global tobacco industry. Altria, the US tobacco group, said on Thursday, November 26, that it was considering a possible all-cash bid for the London-listed maker of Davidoff, Gauloises and Winston cigarettes, and that it was in talks with an undisclosed third party about buying part of Imperial’s business.

The announcement sent Imperial’s shares soaring. They rose as much as 17.6% in London trading on Thursday, closing up almost 15% at £34.32, making the company the biggest riser on the FTSE 100 index. Before Altria disclosed its possible bid, Imperial was valued at roughly £22.77 billion, or about $30 billion; by the end of Thursday, its market value had risen to about £25.9 billion, according to Reuters, and the shares gained another 2% on Friday to £35.03.

No price has been disclosed, and no deal is certain. Altria said any offer, if made, was likely to be entirely in cash, but cautioned that there was no guarantee its deliberations would result in a formal offer. Imperial, for its part, said in a statement that its board had not had any discussions with, or received any approach or proposal from, Altria or the third party, a position it repeated in the following days.

The third party, Bloomberg News reported citing people familiar with the matter, is Japan Tobacco, whose biggest investor is the Japanese state. Analysts believe a deal could be structured so that Altria acquires Imperial’s businesses outside the United States, while Japan Tobacco buys its American operations, a carve-up that could help mitigate antitrust concerns. Shares in Japan Tobacco rose 4.5% in Tokyo on Friday as investors reacted for the first time since the news broke.

Under Britain’s takeover rules, Altria now has until 5pm London time on December 24 to announce a firm intention to make an offer or to walk away, unless regulators grant an extension. The deadline comes from the Takeover Code’s 28-day timetable, which starts running once a potential bidder is publicly identified. Deutsche Bank and Perella Weinberg are advising Altria, according to the company’s regulatory disclosure.

Analysts at Citi said their “base case” was that Altria would acquire Imperial for £39.50 per share, a 30% premium to the price before news of the deal emerged, and simultaneously sell Imperial’s US combustibles business to a third party. “Given the compelling strategic and financial logic, we think the probability of Altria making an offer is high and that Altria shareholders would be supportive,” Citi said, while adding that there was no guarantee Imperial’s management and investors would support it, and that other players could enter the fray.

The logic is driven by a shrinking cigarette market and the rise of alternative nicotine products. Imperial, based in Bristol, makes Lambert & Butler and Davidoff cigarettes as well as Zone nicotine pouches, and published its full-year results on November 17, nine days before Altria’s announcement. Altria, valued at about $124 billion and the owner of the Marlboro brand in the US, has had a strong year: its shares have risen 32% in 2026 on booming demand for oral nicotine pouches, it has raised its dividend for the 57th year in a row, and analysts expect it to generate $9.2 billion of free cash flow this year, money that could help finance an acquisition.

Imperial’s board, led by Thérèse Esperdy, an American former investment banker at JP Morgan, said it was “clear in its duty of aiming to maximise shareholder value and will consider any proposal, should one be made”, adding that it had “strong confidence in its strategy and trading”. The Times reported that analysts believe a takeover “looks strategically sound”, and described Altria as weighing a $30 billion tobacco carve-up in “a race against time”.

The next month will decide it. If Altria makes a firm offer by Christmas Eve, it will be one of the largest tobacco deals ever attempted, and a test of whether regulators on both sides of the Atlantic will accept a carve-up of one of the world’s biggest cigarette makers. If it walks away, Imperial’s shares, which have already given back some of their gains in the days since the announcement, will fall back to earth. Either way, the tobacco industry’s long consolidation is entering a new phase.
