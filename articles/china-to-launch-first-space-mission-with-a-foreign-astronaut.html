China to launch first space mission with a foreign astronaut

Pakistan's Muhammad Zeeshan Ali will become the first foreign astronaut to board China's Tiangong space station when the latest crew blasts off Wednesday. Ali said he would be carrying "the hopes of 250 million Pakistanis" with him.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 24, 2026

China has launched its first space mission to carry a foreign astronaut, sending a Pakistani air force officer to the Tiangong space station in a mission that Beijing and Islamabad are presenting as a milestone in their friendship, and in China’s ambition to open its space program to the world.

The Shenzhou-24 spacecraft lifted off at 8:18 in the morning on Tuesday, November 25, from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center in north-west China, carried by a Long March-2F rocket. About three hours and 38 minutes later, at 11:56am, it docked with the Tiangong space station. By 2:35pm Beijing time, the three crew members had entered the station, where they were greeted with hugs and a cake by the Shenzhou-23 crew already on board.

The crew is led by Tang Hongbo, a veteran commander, with Wu Yi as flight engineer. The third member, and the one who has drawn the attention of the world, is Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, a 40-year-old wing commander in the Pakistan Air Force and a former fighter pilot, who is serving as a payload specialist. He is the first foreign astronaut to fly aboard the Tiangong station, and the first Pakistani to travel into space.

Ali will spend between five and ten days on the station, conducting experiments before returning to Earth aboard Shenzhou-23, which is already docked at the station. The mission is short by the standards of a space station stay, but it is rich in symbolism: for Pakistan, it is a national first; for China, it is the first time it has flown a foreign astronaut to its own station.

The path to the launch began in February 2025, when China and Pakistan signed an agreement on cooperation in human spaceflight. Ali was selected from around 20 finalists through three rounds of selection, alongside a backup candidate, Khurram Dawood. The two men arrived in Beijing on April 24, 2026 for seven months of training that was originally planned to last a year, including Chinese language lessons, and cultural activities that ranged from singing Chinese songs and reciting poetry to learning calligraphy and playing table tennis.

Ali has been open about what the mission means to him. “My first flight represents not only myself, but also my country,” he said before the launch. “I carry on my shoulders the hopes of 250 million Pakistanis.” He also spoke in Chinese to the cameras, saying: “As the first foreign crew member aboard the Chinese space station, I am incredibly excited. China Space Station, here I come!”

The experiments he will conduct are a mix of Pakistani and Chinese science. The payload includes microalgae, which could one day be used to produce food and oxygen in space, and quinoa seeds, which will be studied for how they grow in microgravity. In total, the Shenzhou-24 crew will carry out around 90 experiments during their stay, including work on space medicine, materials science and astronomy.

The reaction in Pakistan was one of celebration. Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif said the mission had taken Pakistan’s flag “beyond the stars,” and that the whole country was proud of Ali. The military leadership, the foreign minister and the planning minister all sent congratulations, and the country’s president and the Chinese president, Xi Jinping, exchanged letters marking the occasion. Xi described the mission as a “living testament” to the deep-rooted friendship between the two countries, which are marking 75 years of diplomatic relations.

The mission also fits into a larger story. Shenzhou-24 is China’s 41st human spaceflight mission and the 680th flight of the Long March rocket family, and it is the eighth crewed flight in the application and development phase of the space station program. China has made no secret of its ambition to open the station to international partners, and the Pakistani mission is the first test of that ambition.

For Ali, the mission is personal as well as national. Before the launch, he received letters from children in Pakistan and from his father, and he has said he wants the mission to inspire young Pakistanis to study science. For China, it is a demonstration that its space station is not just a national project but an international one. And for the rest of the world, it is a reminder that the race to space is no longer a race between two superpowers, it is a race that more and more countries are joining.

Sources: Dawn, Xinhua, the Beijing Review and China Daily.
