Brazil ex-president Jair Bolsonaro taken to hospital over abdominal pain

Jair Bolsonaro has been hospitalized in Brasilia with intestinal obstruction. The former far-right Brazilian leader is receiving pain medications, his son Carlos said.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 21, 2026

Brazil’s former president Jair Bolsonaro is in intensive care in Brasília after being diagnosed with an acute bowel obstruction, in the latest serious health setback for the 71-year-old far-right leader, who is serving a 27-year prison sentence for plotting a coup.

Bolsonaro was admitted on Friday, November 20, to the DF Star hospital in the capital after suffering severe abdominal pain. His son Carlos, a senator-elect, said he was met with a horrifying sight when he arrived: his father was suffering from extremely severe abdominal distension. Tests showed a partial but near-complete intestinal obstruction, his doctor, Brasil Caiado, told journalists.

In a medical bulletin on Saturday, the hospital said doctors had started giving Bolsonaro fluids and nutritional support and had inserted a tube into his stomach. He was described as clinically stable and conscious, breathing on his own, although the hospital said there was no projected date for his discharge. A scan showed damage to his small intestine and signs of inflammation, according to his medical team.

Carlos Bolsonaro described a difficult night of treatment in posts on social media, saying food residue had been removed from his father’s stomach through a tube inserted into his nose, and that he had suffered bouts of nausea. “I’m not a doctor, but I’ve never seen him like this,” he wrote, suggesting that his father might need another operation, which would be his tenth in less than eight years. He later added that there was hope his father’s condition would improve, and that he was “fighting to get his bowels working again, with his usual grit and focus.”

His wife, Michelle Bolsonaro, said on Sunday that he had spent the night without pain and had been able to take a walk. The hospital said he remained clinically stable and was continuing treatment, fasting and nutritional support, without specifying when he might be discharged.

Bolsonaro, who governed Brazil from 2019 to 2022, has suffered repeated abdominal complications since he was stabbed in the stomach during a campaign event in 2018. He has been hospitalised repeatedly and has undergone a series of operations on his abdomen in the years since, and his health has been a recurring factor in his legal situation: he was transferred in March to house arrest after spending several months in jail because of his health problems.

He has been serving a 27-year sentence since last year for an attempted coup after losing the 2022 election to the current president, Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva. Bolsonaro has denied the charges. Next month, Brazil’s Supreme Court is set to consider the constitutionality of a law that changes how sentences are calculated, which could shorten his sentence and those of others convicted in the 2022 coup plot.

The hospitalisation comes at a difficult moment for the Bolsonaro family politically. His eldest son, Flávio Bolsonaro, ran in Brazil’s presidential election last month, losing to Lula, and has since fled to the United States with his immediate family, according to reports. Carlos Bolsonaro, meanwhile, used his father’s hospitalisation to attack the sentence, writing that it was happening “to a man sentenced to 27 years in prison without having committed any crime.”

For now, the medical picture is one of cautious stability rather than recovery. The hospital has said only that Bolsonaro is conscious, stable and under close observation, with doctors yet to decide whether surgery will be needed. Given his history, nine operations in less than eight years, all stemming from the 2018 stabbing, each new abdominal crisis carries a risk that the last one did not, and his doctors have made no promises about when, or in what condition, he will leave the hospital.

The case has also revived the wider argument in Brazil about how a former president convicted of attempting to overturn an election should be treated when his health fails. His supporters present him as a victim of a politicised justice system; his critics note that he is receiving, in a private hospital, the kind of care that many Brazilians cannot access. What neither side disputes is that the man who once dominated Brazilian politics now depends, once again, on surgeons and hospital bulletins.

Sources: Al Jazeera, Reuters, CNN and the DF Star hospital bulletins.
