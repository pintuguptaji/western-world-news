ICE detains Ricardo Larrondo Mendoza and daughter at Seattle airport as video sparks protests

Footage of Saturday incident, filmed by fellow passengers, quickly went viral on social media as mayor calls tactics ‘un-American’.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 23, 2026

Masked immigration agents detained a father and daughter on an airport jet bridge in Seattle on Saturday morning, November 21, as they boarded an Alaska Airlines flight, in a confrontation filmed by fellow passengers that quickly went viral and brought protesters into Seattle-Tacoma International Airport the next day. The flight, Alaska Airlines Flight 1306 bound for Los Angeles, was delayed, and the incident unfolded in front of a crowded boarding queue ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday.

The two people detained were Ricardo Larrondo Mendoza, a Mexican national, and his daughter Sofia Hernandez Mejia, who is in her mid-20s. Video showed officers in unmarked jackets and khaki pants struggling with someone on the ground, passengers screaming, and Mejia shouting that agents were taking her father and that she had a work permit. 'They're taking my dad, we have a work permit. I'm being arrested by ICE,' she can be heard saying.

John Gaedeke, a passenger waiting to board the same flight, filmed part of the confrontation and told the Associated Press that the officers 'just lunged forward. They didn't identify themselves and then sort of hit this family from behind.' He said passengers were screaming or crying at 'the pain and anguish and then the speed and violence with which it happened.' The family's lawyer said the officers, in plain, unmarked clothes, waited until boarding began before approaching suddenly and without identification. The video does not show what happened before the detention began.

According to their lawyer, Olia Catala, Mendoza and Mejia were traveling to California to attend a family baptism ceremony. They have lived in the United States since 2015 and are seeking asylum, Catala told the Associated Press, and neither has a criminal record. 'They've done everything correctly, and these are educated people,' Catala said. 'She went to college, to university, she worked for a nonprofit. No criminal history for any of them.' Mejia had married a US citizen and filed paperwork to adjust her immigration status. Her husband was traveling with them and was not detained.

Immigration and Customs Enforcement gave a different account. In a statement, the agency said its officers had conducted a 'targeted enforcement operation' to arrest Mendoza, whom it described as an immigrant from Mexico who had overstayed his visa 'in violation of our nation's laws'. ICE said that during the arrest, other travellers began to obstruct and assault its officers, and that one passenger 'hurled hot coffee on the officers'. The agency identified that passenger as Mejia, said she had also overstayed her visa, and said she could be charged with assaulting a federal officer.

Catala disputed the agency's version, saying Mejia was carrying a cup of coffee when the confrontation began. 'When somebody's charging at you, you're going to drop a cup of coffee,' she said, adding there was 'a lot of panic, chaos, not knowing what's going on' because the officers were not wearing uniforms. The two accounts, a deliberate assault, or a cup dropped in panic, have not been resolved by any independent account of the first moments.

Alaska Airlines said it was 'concerned' and stressed it does not direct or coordinate immigration enforcement. The airline said ICE gave it no advance notice, and it was working with the Port of Seattle, which manages the airport, to understand what happened. Four of the six crew members scheduled on the flight chose not to fly, according to the MLK Labor union. The Port of Seattle said it was outraged and was asking the Department of Homeland Security whether agreed protocols had been breached.

On Sunday, protesters marched through Seattle-Tacoma International Airport carrying placards reading 'ICE out of Washington' and 'Abolish ICE', chanting 'ICE out now' inside and around the terminal. Photographs showed demonstrators with bullhorns walking through the airport a day after the arrests, in a protest organized in response to the viral video. Further anti-ICE protests were held later in the day in Seattle.

The political reaction was swift. Washington Governor Bob Ferguson, a Democrat, called the tactics 'deeply un-American' and said ICE was 'completely and totally out of control'. 'The video is horrific and it is chilling,' Ferguson said at a news conference on Saturday, and he called for the agency's abolition. Seattle Mayor Katie Wilson said she was 'outraged' by the footage, calling the tactics 'unacceptable and un-American' and saying Seattle would continue to stand as a sanctuary city. Representative Adam Smith called ICE's actions 'absolutely unacceptable', and Representative Pramila Jayapal said 'violent arrests are the hallmark of fascist states, not a democracy.'

As of Sunday, Mendoza and Mejia were being held at the Northwest ICE Processing Center in Tacoma, about 40 minutes south of Seattle, one of the largest immigration detention centres in the country, with roughly 1,600 beds, according to their lawyer. What happens next depends on their asylum claims and Mejia's application to adjust her status through her marriage to a US citizen, any criminal charge arising from the alleged coffee incident, and the inquiries by the Port of Seattle and Alaska Airlines. ICE has stepped up arrests at airports since the summer, as the Trump administration pushes to raise deportation numbers. For the passengers who filmed the struggle, the central question is not only whether the detentions were lawful, but whether an airport boarding queue is a place where such an arrest should ever happen.
