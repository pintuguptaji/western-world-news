Kilmar Ábrego García leaving US for Spain, ending Trump deportation battle

His deportation to El Salvador and return to the US became a flash point in the debate over President Donald Trump's immigration agenda.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 25, 2026

Kilmar Ábrego García, the Salvadoran man whose wrongful deportation became one of the defining sagas of President Donald Trump's immigration crackdown, has left the United States voluntarily for Spain, traveling, as he put it, with his freedom, to a country of his choosing.

Ábrego García, 31, departed on Tuesday on a United Airlines flight out of Washington Dulles International Airport, after agents removed the GPS monitor he had worn on his ankle for months. He landed in Madrid early on Wednesday with his wife, Jennifer Vasquez Sura, and his brother at his side. Outside the terminal, he said nothing to the cameras waiting for him. Before stepping into a van, he raised a fist.

He entered Spain on a tourist visa, allowing a visit of only up to 90 days. Hours after his arrival, he spoke for himself for the first time since landing, in Spanish, at a virtual news conference, casting his departure not as an exile but as the end of a 20-month ordeal he had survived on his own terms. “The government does not decide where my story continues,” he said. “This time I am leaving with my freedom,” he added, a sharp contrast with the first time he was forced out of the country, in spring 2025, “in a practically inhumane manner”.

The terms of his exit were laid out in an order US District Judge Paula Xinis signed on November 20 and unsealed five days later. It required the government to lift any holds that might stop him at the gate and barred officials from diverting him anywhere other than the country he picked, a safeguard against the third-country deportations the administration had pursued for months. The order also specifies that his departure “shall constitute execution of his order of removal”, meaning the government will record it as a deportation, a designation that could complicate any attempt to return.

The story behind the departure is by now familiar, and extraordinary. Ábrego García was arrested in Baltimore in March 2025 and deported to a Salvadoran mega-prison despite a 2019 court order barring him from being sent to El Salvador because of the risk of persecution. The Trump administration later described the deportation as an “administrative error”, but initially refused to seek his return, arguing that he was a member of the MS-13 gang, despite his having no criminal record at the time of his removal. He has never been convicted of a crime or charged with gang membership.

In June 2025, the administration brought him back after facing public pressure and a court order, but only after securing an indictment charging him with human smuggling in Tennessee. That case was dismissed by a federal judge in May, though the government appealed it in August. His case, at one point, reached the US Supreme Court.

What followed was a months-long fight over where he would be sent next. Ábrego García had said that if he must be deported, he would prefer Costa Rica, a country that has said it would be willing to give him some form of legal status. But Todd Lyons, the former acting director of US Immigration and Customs Enforcement, wrote in a March 2026 memo that deporting him to Costa Rica would be “prejudicial to the United States”, and argued instead that he should be sent to Liberia, because the US had spent government resources and political capital negotiating for it to accept third-country nationals. Uganda, Eswatini and Ghana were also proposed. The case took an unusual turn on October 6, when, shortly before a hearing to determine whether ICE could deport him to Liberia, attorneys for both sides left the courtroom with Ábrego García.

The reactions to his departure split along the same lines as the case itself. The Department of Homeland Security announced it on Tuesday, with Secretary Markwayne Mullin writing on social media that he was “delighted” by the move to self-deport, and repeating the administration's familiar allegations against him. US Senator Chris Van Hollen, who met with Ábrego García while pressing for his release from the Salvadoran prison, said the 31-year-old was “traveling to a country of his choice, a free man”, and added: “I am relieved that he will be safe, in a country of his choosing, and free, including from this Administration's constant persecution.”

His lawyer, Simon Sandoval-Moshenberg, described the move as “a temporary departure”, noting that Ábrego García has many options to return to the United States as the husband of a US citizen spouse and the father of a US citizen child. “He has now obtained the precise result that the government was offering him in exchange for a coerced guilty plea,” Sandoval-Moshenberg said, and, he added, “he's going to outlast this administration. Kilmar Ábrego García is going to last a lot longer than Donald Trump. And he will be back in the United States as a lawful permanent resident under the next administration.”

Ábrego García is scheduled to speak at several events in Spain and other countries in the days ahead. His departure closes, or at least pauses, a legal fight that made him one of the most recognisable faces of the administration's deportation campaign, and whether he returns one day, as his lawyer predicts, is a question for the years ahead.

Sources: Al Jazeera, USA Today, CNN and the Associated Press.
