Shanghai named the world's best shopping city, and Europe has just six entries in the top 20

Europe may be home to three of the world’s fashion capitals, but it has just six cities in Time Out’s top 20 shopping destinations ranking, with only London and Copenhagen making the top 10.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 27, 2026

Shanghai has been named the world's best shopping city in a new global ranking by Time Out, published in partnership with Intrepid Travel. The Chinese megacity topped the first edition of Time Out's Best Cities for Shopping list, ahead of London, Tokyo, New York and Melbourne, in a result that underlines how far the center of gravity in global retail has shifted towards Asia.

The ranking draws on Time Out's annual Best Cities survey, which collected responses from more than 24,000 residents across 150 cities, combined with the views of the publication's global network of editors and local experts. The scoring model gives 70 percent of the weight to local sentiment and 30 percent to expert evaluation, so the list reflects what the people who actually live and shop in these cities think, rather than the judgement of visitors or travel writers alone.

Shanghai's victory was attributed to the breadth of its shopping scene, which brings together international fashion brands, independent retailers, local designers, antique markets and specialist shopping districts. Local expert Sammi Sowerby described the city's shopping scene as being at its zenith, something she attributed to the cultural phenomenon known as guochao, or national tide, the growing pride in homegrown Chinese brands. Time Out's recommendations include local labels such as An Ko Rau, The Beast, Pane and Short Sentence, reflecting a shopping scene that balances global reach with a distinctly local identity.

London took second place, supported by a retail landscape that ranges from luxury department stores to independent shops and vintage markets. Time Out London's editor recommended Daunt Books in Marylebone as a quintessential stop, alongside Harrods, Liberty and Selfridges on Bond Street, Brick Lane's vintage stalls and Soho's record shops. Tokyo came third, with its world-renowned fashion districts, second-hand shopping scene and abundance of specialist retailers, followed by New York in fourth and Melbourne in fifth.

Asian cities dominate the ranking. Five of the top ten places are Asian: Shanghai, Tokyo, Seoul in sixth, Ho Chi Minh City in seventh and Bangkok in eighth. Mexico City, in ninth, and Copenhagen, in tenth, are the only other non-Asian cities in the top ten. The pattern is a striking one for a list of the world's shopping capitals, and it suggests that the most dynamic retail in the world is now to be found in Asia's big cities rather than in the traditional fashion capitals of Europe.

Europe, by contrast, has a surprisingly small presence. The continent is home to three of the four traditional global fashion capitals, yet it claims just six places in the top 20, and only London and Copenhagen make the top ten. Lisbon is eleventh, Barcelona sixteenth, Florence seventeenth and Vienna eighteenth. Paris, Milan and Rome, three of the most famous shopping cities in the world, do not appear in the top 20 at all.

The rest of the top 20 underlines the list's global spread. Montreal is twelfth, Marrakech thirteenth, Abu Dhabi fourteenth, Cape Town fifteenth, and Bogota nineteenth, with Hong Kong twentieth. From spice markets in Marrakech to vintage boutiques in Copenhagen and concept stores in Seoul, the list rewards cities where shopping is an experience woven into daily life, rather than a row of identical luxury flagships.

Time Out's editors made the point explicitly. From Shanghai's homegrown fashion labels and Seoul's immersive concept stores to Copenhagen's vintage boutiques and Marrakech's traditional artisans, they said, each city serves up a unique shopping experience that is well worth planning a trip around. The message is that the best shopping city is not necessarily the one with the most expensive shops, but the one where local makers, markets and independent retailers give a place its own character.

The ranking is Time Out's first dedicated to shopping, and it sits alongside the publication's wider Best Cities survey, which asks residents to rate their cities on nightlife, culture, food, accessibility and general atmosphere. In that broader survey, Melbourne was named the best city in the world for 2026. The shopping list, though, tells its own story, and it is a story about Asia's rise and Europe's relative decline as a retail destination.

For Europe's fashion capitals, the result is a warning as much as a ranking. London held its own in second place, and Copenhagen, Lisbon, Barcelona, Florence and Vienna all made the list. But the cities setting the pace in 2026 are Shanghai, Tokyo and Seoul, where local brands, street markets and new retail formats are drawing shoppers from around the world. The world's best city for shopping, it turns out, is not in Europe at all.
