Mamdani announces plan to close New York's Rikers Island jail by 2032

Plans to shutter the troubled facility date back to 2019, when the city council first voted to close it but delays arose over where to place inmates.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 21, 2026

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani has unveiled a plan to close the notorious Rikers Island jail complex by 2032, setting out how his administration hopes to shut down a facility long criticised for violence, overcrowding and poor sanitation.

The plan, released on Friday, November 20, is the city's first comprehensive strategy for closing Rikers, a 75-page document titled “Close Rikers: Strategic Plan” that lays out the steps the city will take to build a safer and more humane jail system. It proposes closing the jail five years after a deadline of August 2027 that was set under then-Mayor Bill de Blasio, and replacing it with four smaller borough-based jails.

“It is time to finally close Rikers Island,” Mamdani said. “For decades, New Yorkers have demanded a correctional system that is safe and humane. We have a legal and moral obligation to close Rikers,” he added, announcing the plan at a news conference at City Hall alongside Department of Correction Commissioner Stanley Richards, the first formerly incarcerated person to lead the department.

The strategy has three moving parts. First, the construction timetable: the first replacement jail will open in Brooklyn in 2029, followed by facilities in Queens and the Bronx in 2031 and Manhattan in 2032, each with 1,040 beds. Second, the population must come down: the plan aims to reduce the number of people held at Rikers from around 6,700 to no more than 4,100 by the end of 2032, including no more than 220 women, a reduction of roughly 2,500 people. Third, the investment: the administration will spend $146 million next fiscal year on mental health care, substance-use treatment, education and job training, both inside jails and in communities, as well as services for crime victims.

Getting the population down is the hardest part. The mayor's strategy involves expanding alternatives to detention, restoring online bail payments, investing in programmes that aim to reduce repeat offending, and shortening court delays. Court delays can leave people “detained for years in what is essentially legal purgatory”, Mamdani said at the news conference, and the city plans to set up a task force to deal with bottlenecks and improve how agencies share evidence. The administration will also create 236 additional Outposted Therapeutic Housing Units in Brooklyn and the Bronx, for a total of 340 therapeutic beds in city hospitals for people in custody with serious mental health conditions and complex medical needs.

Rikers Island, near LaGuardia Airport in New York's East River, opened in 1932 and has long faced criticism over violence, abuse and unsanitary conditions. The complex mostly holds people awaiting trial rather than serving sentences after conviction. It has been tied to cases like that of Kalief Browder, who spent three years there awaiting trial before the charges against him were dismissed; he died by suicide in 2015, aged 22, two years after his release. Campaigners have pushed for its closure for over a decade, and city lawmakers voted to close it in 2019.

That 2019 vote set the August 2027 deadline the city is now certain to miss. With less than a year to go, the four smaller jails meant to replace Rikers are still under construction, and Mamdani has blamed the delay on de-prioritisation under the previous Adams administration, as well as the Covid-19 pandemic. The effort to close Rikers was first championed in 1979, and the new plan builds on de Blasio's original proposal for four modern borough-based jails closer to courthouses, public transport and the communities where incarcerated New Yorkers live.

The plan's most controversial portion is the delay itself. The mayor cannot change the closure deadline on his own: the City Council will have to pass legislation to legally extend it to 2032, and the proposal is expected to set off a new round of negotiations. The administration says it will work with the Council, the remediation manager, the federal monitor and the Board of Correction to advance it. It is also likely to face opposition from local communities and from the union representing correction officers, which has consistently pushed to keep Rikers open.

Mamdani, who appointed a “Close Rikers Czar” and chose Richards to lead the Department of Correction after taking office, framed the plan in human terms. “Any family member of someone held at Rikers will tell you how isolating that jail is, how it can take hours to visit a loved one,” he said, adding that the new jails would be closer to courts and public transport and would help integrate imprisoned New Yorkers back into the city. Once the jail closes, he wants the island to be used for renewable energy and projects to help the city withstand the effects of climate change. “Our hope is that the new Rikers Island will be unrecognisable,” he said.

Whether the timetable holds is an open question, the project carries a price tag of roughly $16 billion and a history of delays. But for the first time, the city has a published, comprehensive plan with dates, bed numbers and a budget attached. The next test is the City Council, and whether it will legislate the five-year extension that makes the whole plan possible.

Sources: Al Jazeera, the City of New York, CNN and the New York Post.
