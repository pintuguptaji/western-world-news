Reporters Without Borders demands suspension of controversial French TV channel CNews

Paris-based international press freedom group Reporters Without Borders (RSF) on Thursday called on France's media regulator Arcom to suspend right-wing channel CNews for one month over its failure to uphold pluralism.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 26, 2026

Reporters Without Borders (RSF) has asked France's media regulator to suspend the right-wing news channel CNews for one month, accusing it of systematically favouring the far right and failing in its legal duty to uphold pluralism, five months before France's presidential election.

The Paris-based international press freedom group wrote to Arcom, the Audiovisual and Digital Communication Regulatory Authority, on Thursday November 26. Its director-general, Thibaut Bruttin, said there was a danger for democracy that was becoming more evident as the country approached the presidential election, and argued that press freedom also meant being able to distinguish between what amounted to brainwashing and what was genuine, free and independent journalism.

RSF's case rests on an analysis of 61 hours of programming from three of CNews' most-watched shows. Its investigators concluded that immigration, insecurity, Islamism, identity issues, criticism of the left and criticism of the public media accounted for two-thirds of the topics debated on the channel. In its report, RSF found that nearly 90 per cent of the opinions expressed on the programmes converged in the same direction, feeding a dominant narrative that left only a residual place for contradiction and nuance.

The group says the pattern is deliberate rather than accidental. In an investigation in 2025, RSF concluded that CNews used its overnight slots, when few people watch, to broadcast more left-leaning content in order to satisfy regulatory demands for balanced programming, while its peak-time output remained heavily one-sided. Arnaud Froger, head of RSF's investigation desk, said the request for suspension was the result of a long campaign, arguing that fines did not solve the problem and that Arcom's regulatory policy had failed.

Arcom told AFP that it was investigating and suspected a breach of the rules governing radio and television channels that oblige news broadcasters to offer a variety of political points of view. The regulator said elements of RSF's referral that proved relevant could be added to the file to be examined by an independent rapporteur before any possible sanction. Arcom had already begun monitoring compliance with pluralism of opinion on France's four news channels in September.

CNews, owned by conservative billionaire Vincent Bollore through the Canal+ group, is seen by its critics as a partisan far-right outlet. Its supporters argue that it has helped address alleged left-wing bias in the French media and praise its focus on crime and immigration. The channel condemned RSF's call in the strongest terms, denouncing what it called the group's relentless attacks, a questionable methodology, a challenge to editorial freedom and an activist discourse. Canal+ has denied allegations of bias in the past, and other media groups in the Bollore empire have accused RSF of trying to censor their journalism.

The channel is no stranger to the regulator. According to RSF research, CNews has been handed 12 warnings and 11 fines from Arcom, totalling 830,000 euros, since its creation in 2017. RSF has regularly urged the regulator to take firmer action against the channel since 2021, and says the accumulation of warnings and fines shows that financial penalties have not changed its editorial line.

The referral comes amid a wider campaign against the channel. A citizen petition calling for CNews to be banned exceeded one million signatures after a racist attack in the Paris suburb of Trappes on October 23, although the National Assembly dismissed the petition. A national mobilisation under the slogan Unplug CNews is being organized for December 12 to demand that Arcom withdraw the channel's broadcast license so it can be reallocated to a channel that respects its commitments.

The timing matters because France is heading into a presidential election, with the two rounds scheduled for April 18 and May 2, 2027. Far-right leader Marine Le Pen is currently leading in opinion polls, and RSF argues that a channel reaching a large audience with heavily one-sided coverage in the run-up to the vote poses a particular risk. CNews' defenders reply that voters, not regulators, should judge its output, and that suspending a news channel would itself be an act of censorship.

A one-month suspension would be an unprecedented sanction in the French audiovisual landscape, and it could only follow an investigation lasting several months. RSF describes the measure it is seeking as a dissuasive and credible sanction, but even its supporters acknowledge that Arcom moves slowly and that the channel is likely to remain on air through the election campaign. The case has nevertheless become a test of how far France's regulator is willing to go to enforce pluralism, and of whether a press freedom organisation can credibly demand the silencing, however temporary, of a news outlet.
