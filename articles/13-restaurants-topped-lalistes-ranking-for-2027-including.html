13 restaurants topped LaListe’s ranking for 2027, including seven in Europe

L’Assiette Champenoise in Tinqueux, France, earned a 99.5 score, reserved for the best restaurants in the world, for the first time this year.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 25, 2026

Thirteen restaurants share first place in La Liste's 2027 ranking of the world's best restaurants, each earning a score of 99.5 out of 100. The list was unveiled in Paris on Monday, November 23, and seven of the thirteen top-ranked restaurants are in Europe, including two in France.

The thirteen are Cheval Blanc by Peter Knogl in Basel, Switzerland; Da Vittorio in Brusaporto, Italy; Frantzén in Stockholm, Sweden; Guy Savoy in Paris, France; L'Assiette Champenoise in Tinqueux, France; Le Bernardin in New York; Martín Berasategui in Lasarte, Spain; Schwarzwaldstube in Baiersbronn, Germany; SingleThread in Healdsburg, California; Robuchon au Dôme in Macao; Matsukawa and Sushi Saito in Tokyo, Japan; and Lung King Heen in Hong Kong.

For L'Assiette Champenoise, the top step is a first. Chef Arnaud Lallement's restaurant in Tinqueux, a suburb just outside Reims, gained half a point to reach 99.5. Lallement has held three Michelin stars at the restaurant since 2014, and his signature blue lobster dish is a tribute to his father, who passed the restaurant down to him. The other newcomer at the top is Sushi Saito in Tokyo, while Björn Frantzén's Stockholm restaurant returns to the summit after a gap.

Guy Savoy, whose restaurant sits on the banks of the Seine in Paris, holds his share of first place for a tenth consecutive year, despite losing his third Michelin star. La Liste's founder and chief executive, Philippe Faure, summed up the changes at the top: "Thirteen restaurants share first place this year. L'Assiette Champenoise and Sushi Saito are new at the top, and Frantzén is back."

La Liste, founded in Paris in 2015 and presided over by Faure, takes a different approach from the Michelin Guide or the World's 50 Best list. Instead of sending out its own inspectors, it aggregates scores from more than 1,000 guides, press reviews and online sources, weighting each by reliability and reach, and folds in online guest reviews, which count for 10 percent of the final score. The result is a single number out of 100 for each restaurant. Nobody has ever reached 100, and this year thirteen came within half a point of it.

France dominates the wider ranking. The country places 33 restaurants in the top 200 and 118 addresses across the full list of 1,000. Just below the summit, at 99, Paris holds two more leading tables, Yannick Alléno's Pavillon Ledoyen and Plénitude, while Kei and Le Pré Catelan complete a very Parisian top tier at 98.5. Eighteen restaurants in total scored 99, including Alain Ducasse's Le Louis XV in Monaco and Arnaud Donckele's La Vague d'Or in Saint-Tropez.

The 2027 edition was unveiled at the Quai d'Orsay in Paris and draws on sources across 74 countries, with some 35,000 restaurants considered for the top 1,000. Beyond the thirteen at the top, the list reaches deep into national dining scenes: Melbourne's Vue de monde, for example, was the only Australian restaurant among the highest ranked, scoring 97 out of 100. The top 1,000 are determined from around 1,200 sources, including restaurant guides, media critics and diner reviews, a breadth that La Liste says makes the ranking democratic, even if it can never be definitive.

Faure used the launch to argue that great cooking is not only found at the most expensive tables. "Great gastronomy is not defined by the size of the bill," he said. "With our new European selections, we show that it is possible to eat remarkably well at reasonable prices."

A place on the list matters beyond prestige. For restaurants, a La Liste score can mean fuller dining rooms, stronger bookings and more visitors to their towns, and the tied top scores reflect the guide's method of aggregating many opinions rather than crowning a single juried winner. For diners, the 2027 ranking offers a map of where fine dining's center of gravity sits today, with Japan, the United States and France each placing two restaurants in the circle of thirteen.
