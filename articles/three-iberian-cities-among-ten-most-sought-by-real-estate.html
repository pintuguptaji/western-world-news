Three Iberian cities among ten most sought by real estate investors in Europe in 2027

According to Colliers' report for next year, Madrid, Lisbon and Barcelona rank among the ten European cities most sought by investors, at a time when protests over access to housing are spreading across the Iberian Peninsula.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 26, 2026

Three cities on the Iberian Peninsula are among the ten most sought-after by international real estate investors in Europe for 2027, according to the annual Global Investor Outlook published by Colliers. Madrid ranks third, and Lisbon and Barcelona are tied in ninth place, in a ranking that covers the Europe, Middle East and Africa region and is led by Milan, followed by London and Paris, which share second place.

Colliers, which specialises in commercial real estate, engineering and investment management, publishes the outlook each year to analyse the strategies of property investors worldwide. The ranking reflects the cities that international investors say they intend to target with capital commitments next year, rather than a measure of current prices or transaction volumes. For Iberia, the result confirms a position the region has been building for several years.

Madrid's third place puts it behind only Milan and the two shared runners-up, London and Paris. Lisbon and Barcelona, tied in ninth, round out the Iberian presence in the top ten. Pedro Valente, Colliers' managing director in Portugal, said Lisbon's position highlighted the attractiveness of the Portuguese capital in a European context in which investors continue to identify opportunities, albeit with an increasingly selective approach, favouring assets capable of generating income growth, with solid fundamentals and limited supply.

The report also shows what kinds of property investors want. In the EMEA region, the industrial and logistics sector tops investor preferences, accounting for 17 percent of choices, followed by apartment buildings and offices, both at 15 percent, ahead of hotels at 13 percent and retail at 12 percent. Colliers says this reflects the importance of housing in European markets, where supply is tight, construction and financing costs are high, and new projects are difficult to develop.

Luke Dawson, Colliers' head of global and EMEA capital markets, said investors should focus on assets where returns can grow, supply is effectively limited, and local and macroeconomic fundamentals justify the cost of capital. The report points to returns increasingly linked to rental growth, active management of property assets and greater selectivity in investment decisions. Lisbon, Colliers says, has high visibility among international capital for 2027, in a market where the ability to increase revenues, asset quality, the availability of infrastructure and pricing discipline will be decisive in converting investor appetite into transactions.

The ranking is not just a list of attractive cities. It is an indicator of where capital will flow next year, and it arrives at a moment of tension in the very cities it celebrates. According to Colliers' own reporting, the Iberian cities rank among the most sought-after at a time when protests over access to housing are spreading across the Iberian Peninsula. In Lisbon, Barcelona and Madrid, residents have taken to the streets over rising rents and house prices, and the same constrained supply that attracts investors is what makes homes unaffordable for the people who live there.

A separate survey tells a similar story, though with different rankings. The Emerging Trends in Real Estate Europe 2027 report, published jointly by PwC and the Urban Land Institute from a survey of senior property professionals, puts London first for the sixth consecutive year, followed by Madrid in second, Paris in third, Munich in fourth and Milan in fifth, with Barcelona eighth and Lisbon tenth. The two surveys measure different things, one asking investors where they intend to commit capital, the other asking professionals about overall prospects, but both place Madrid and Lisbon among Europe's strongest cities for 2027.

For the Iberian cities, the challenge is to turn investor interest into buildings, jobs and homes, rather than simply higher prices. Colliers' emphasis on income growth and asset quality suggests that investors are looking for properties that can earn more, not just be sold for more. Whether that translates into new housing, offices and logistics space, or into further pressure on prices, will depend on how much gets built, and how quickly.

The outlook for 2027 is, in Colliers' view, a selective one. Capital is available, but it is choosy, favouring markets with strong fundamentals and limited supply. Iberia, on that measure, qualifies. Madrid, Lisbon and Barcelona are among the ten cities international investors most want to be in next year. The question for the year ahead is whether the people who already live there will benefit from the attention, or be priced out by it.
