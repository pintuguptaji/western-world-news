Japan stun Brazil 3-2 in Moriyasu's farewell friendly

A stoppage-time winner from Tatsuhito Sakamoto sealed victory in Singapore, a final win for the coach who has led Japan for eight years.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 16, 2026

Japan stunned Brazil 3-2 in a thrilling international friendly in Singapore on Saturday November 14, with substitute Tatsuhiro Sakamoto scoring a stoppage-time winner in coach Hajime Moriyasu's farewell international window after eight years in charge.

The Samurai Blue seized the early initiative in front of 38,688 fans at the National Stadium, scoring twice in a four-minute spell. On 15 minutes, a through ball by Ritsu Doan was picked up just inside the Brazilian half by Takefusa Kubo, who showed quick feet to cut inside defender Jair before firing a low effort past goalkeeper Pedro Morisco. Japan added a second shortly afterwards, after fine work by Shuto Nakano, who broke down the right flank and centred for an unmarked Keito Nakamura to calmly place a right-footed shot into the bottom corner.

Brazil, who had gone close through Estevao in the sixth minute, responded with a moment of individual brilliance. Shortly after the half-hour mark, Vinicius Junior collected a pass from Bruno Guimaraes outside the area and fired an unstoppable shot into the top corner to make it 2-1 before the break. Japan goalkeeper Zion Suzuki had already denied Estevao when the winger was clear on goal, and Daizen Maeda had a shot saved and put the rebound wide in the minutes before Nakamura's goal.

The match turned fractious before half-time. Brazil coach Carlo Ancelotti was furious when Raphinha appeared to be pushed in the box in the 43rd minute and no penalty was given, and the Brazil captain had already reacted angrily to the decision. Suzuki saved from Raphinha early in the second half, and clear chances were scarce until the 75th minute, when Vinicius cut in from the left and curled another long-range effort that struck the inside of the right post and crossed the line off the left post to level the score at 2-2.

Brazil pushed for a winner and Endrick, on as a substitute after Raphinha was replaced in the 71st minute, smashed a shot off the crossbar from distance four minutes after the equaliser. But it was Japan who found the decisive goal. In the first minute of added time, a deflected shot from Kodai Sano dropped to Sakamoto, a winger for Coventry City, who pounced on the loose ball and lobbed the goalkeeper from close range to score his first goal for Japan.

Brazil protested that there had been a foul on Guimaraes in the build-up to the winner, and the protests boiled over. Ancelotti and Raphinha, who had already been substituted and was watching from the sidelines, were both shown red cards on the touchline by Singaporean referee Jansen Foo. Raphinha remonstrated with the fourth official as he left the field, and there were further protests from Brazil players after the final whistle, with security staff stepping in. Ancelotti said afterwards that Foo had sent him off because he could not control his bench, but that he was otherwise content with his team's performance, saying they had played with the intensity he wanted.

The win was revenge for Japan. Brazil had eliminated them 2-1 in the round of 32 at the World Cup in June, and Japan, ranked 17th in the world, appeared intent on avenging that defeat from the first whistle. It was Japan's second win over Brazil in 13 months, after they had also beaten the five-time world champions 3-2 in a friendly last year, and their first victory over Brazil on foreign soil. It was also only their second win in 16 meetings between the teams, and it came in the last international window of 2026.

For Moriyasu, 58, the night was a fitting farewell. He has led Japan since 2018 and will step down after the friendly against Paraguay on Tuesday, when he hands over to successor Go Oiwa. He described the performance against Brazil as the best of his tenure, saying through a translator that his players had overcome what they could not manage against Brazil at the World Cup, and that he was really satisfied with how they had played. Japan converted three of their eight shots, with Kubo, Nakamura and Sakamoto each scoring with their only shot on goal.

For Brazil, the defeat added to the pressure on Ancelotti, who became coach in 2025 and signed a contract extension through the 2030 World Cup earlier this year. Brazilian fans criticised the team on social media after the game, and there were plenty of complaints about the refereeing. Brazil are due to face Singapore in another friendly on November 17, and the team will need to assess Raphinha's availability after his dismissal.

Japan, meanwhile, face Paraguay next as they build towards the next World Cup, with Moriyasu's final match in charge to come on Tuesday. The Brazil win will be remembered as the high point of his farewell: a two-goal lead, a Brazilian comeback led by one of the world's best players, and a stoppage-time winner from a substitute scoring his first international goal. It was, as Moriyasu himself said, the best match of his eight years.
