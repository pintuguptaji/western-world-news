Israeli strikes on Gaza kill senior Hamas security official and six other people, medics say

Israeli air strikes on the devastated Gaza Strip killed Brigadier General Naeem Abu Nahel, director-general of the Palestinian territory's Internal Security Service on Tuesday, the ministry said.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 24, 2026

Israeli air strikes on Gaza on Tuesday killed Naim Abu Nahal, the director of Hamas’s internal security apparatus, along with six other people, medics said, in the latest deadly day under a ceasefire that has been fraying for weeks.

Abu Nahal, 50, was killed by a drone strike as he walked in the street in the Ansar area, southwest of Gaza City, according to Gaza’s interior ministry and Palestinian reports. Several civilians were wounded in the strike, and Gaza’s civil defense spokesman, Mahmoud Bassal, said several other people were in critical condition.

Abu Nahal, whose full name was given by the Israeli military as Naim Muhammad Ashour Abu Nahal, had held the post only since September, when he replaced Moein al-Arbeed after his predecessor was captured by Israeli forces. He had previously served as deputy head of the apparatus, which is responsible for counterespionage, protecting senior Hamas officials and vital facilities, and investigations into security and public order in Gaza.

The Israeli military and the Shin Bet domestic security service said in a joint statement that they had struck a senior Hamas figure, without initially identifying him, adding that further details would be provided later. The military later published footage showing the strike on Abu Nahal as he walked down a street. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Defense Minister Israel Katz then confirmed in a joint statement that he had been killed.

The military said Abu Nahal had held several senior roles in the internal security apparatus and was “a senior figure with extensive experience in the organization and served as a significant source of knowledge”. It said his killing “deals a blow to the apparatus’s management capabilities”. The military justified the strike by saying he had recently been advancing attacks on Israeli troops and civilians and was acting to restore Hamas’s capabilities “while systematically violating the ceasefire”.

Hamas condemned the strike as an assassination. The group has insisted that it is being hunted regardless of the ceasefire, while Israel maintains that the truce has already been broken by Hamas’s efforts to rebuild its armed capacity, a dispute that has left the ceasefire looking increasingly hollow on the ground.

The six other people killed on Tuesday included two police officers and a 17-year-old, according to medics cited by France 24. A source in Gaza’s rescue services told Al Jazeera that three people, including a child, were killed in a strike on a police post west of Khan Younis. Haaretz and Al Jazeera also reported that five people were killed in a strike on an apartment in the Shati refugee camp in western Gaza City on Tuesday night, among them Mohammed Abu al-Aouf, who headed Hamas’s internal security apparatus in northern Gaza, his wife and their two daughters.

Reports differed on the day’s overall toll: medics cited by France 24 put the number of dead at seven, while Haaretz reported at least ten killed across the strip. The discrepancy reflects the difficulty of verifying casualty figures in Gaza, where counts are compiled by medical and rescue services working under Hamas authority and updated through the day.

The strikes came despite a ceasefire in place since last year, which ended the major fighting but has not stopped targeted killings or air strikes. Abu Nahal is the second senior internal security chief to be lost by Hamas in a matter of months: his predecessor was captured by Israeli forces in Gaza City in September, in what Netanyahu and Katz described as a daring operation.

For Israel, the killing removes a man it described as a central source of knowledge inside Hamas’s security apparatus at a moment when it accuses the group of rebuilding. For Hamas, it is another blow to the organisation that polices Gaza internally, the body that hunts collaborators and protects its leadership. With each side accusing the other of violating the truce, and no sign of a renewed agreement to halt the strikes, the episode points to a ceasefire surviving in name while the war of attrition around it continues.
