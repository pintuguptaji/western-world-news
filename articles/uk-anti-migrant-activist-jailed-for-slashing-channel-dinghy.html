UK anti-migrant activist jailed for slashing Channel dinghy

Daniel Thomas, known as "Danny Tommo," slashed a dinghy with a knife while a French rescue worker was on board. The incident was livestreamed to a large social media audience amid growing tensions over migrant crossings.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 21, 2026

WWNEurope

A British anti-migrant activist has been jailed for 15 months after he used a knife to slash a small boat carrying migrants across the English Channel, an attack a judge described as a deliberate and publicity-seeking act of interference with a rescue operation.

Danny Tommo, 37, was sentenced on Friday, November 20, at Portsmouth Crown Court, appearing by video link from HMP Winchester. He had admitted criminal damage. The sentence would have been 20 months had he gone to trial, the judge said, but was reduced because of his guilty plea.

The incident took place on September 22. Tommo, who runs a social media account called Patriot Platform, followed two small boats carrying migrants as they left northern France, and steered his own boat towards a dinghy that had been left drifting after the migrants on board had been removed by the French authorities. A French rescuer was still on board the dinghy when Tommo reached it.

He slashed the boat about eight times with a knife, the court heard, leaving a hole and, in the judge’s words, “a hissing sound as the air escaped.” The attack was livestreamed on social media, with Tommo shouting “This is England, this is not France any more” and “this is what needs to happen to every one of these boats” as he cut into the inflatable. The Royal National Lifeboat Institution, the UK Border Force and the French Coastguard had all been involved in removing the migrants from the boat before Tommo reached it.

Judge James Newton-Price KC was scathing. He described Tommo as a “publicity seeker” who had engaged in “flagrant interference and deliberate disruption” and who had “intended to have an impact on a live audience.” He accepted that the French rescuer was not in immediate danger, but said the attack was a serious interference with a rescue operation and with the work of the authorities.

The court heard that the dinghy did not sink, and made its way back to France. It was later destroyed by the French authorities, which meant that the value of the damage could not be established. Tommo’s lawyer, Simon Pentol KC, told the court that the knife was part of his client’s equipment and that he had not intended to endanger anyone, an argument the judge did not accept.

Tommo is a well-known figure in Britain’s anti-migrant movement. He has organized protests in Portsmouth and Dover, and his social media accounts have a large following among people who oppose Channel crossings. His supporters presented the case as an example of a man being punished for protesting, while his critics said the sentence was lenient for an attack on a vessel in the middle of the Channel.

The case is not over. Tommo is due to stand trial in June 2027 at Westminster Magistrates’ Court in connection with separate offences in Dover, which he denies, and a further charge relating to his refusal to give the police the PIN for his phone was ordered to lie on file.

Beyond Tommo himself, the case matters for the organisations that work in the Channel. The Royal National Lifeboat Institution, the Border Force and the French Coastguard are involved in rescues almost every day, and the operation on September 22 was a routine one, until Tommo’s boat arrived. The judge’s ruling makes clear that interfering with such an operation, whatever the motive, is a criminal offence, and that livestreaming it to an audience makes it worse, not better.

For the authorities, the case sends a message: interfering with rescue operations in the Channel, however it is framed, is a crime, and it will be punished. For Tommo’s supporters, it confirms their view that the state protects the boats and punishes those who try to stop them. The judge’s words, though, were aimed at something simpler, that a man who livestreamed himself slashing a boat in the Channel, with a rescuer on board, was not making a protest. He was making a show.

Sources: the BBC and Al Jazeera.
