Czech Republic ready to support Poland after Russian airspace violations

Prague's offer comes after Russian weapons entered Polish airspace during a large-scale attack on western Ukraine on November 20.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 25, 2026

The Czech Republic is ready to support Poland after Russian weapons entered Polish airspace during a large-scale attack on western Ukraine on November 20. The offer was announced by Defense Minister Jaromír Zůna and Chief of the General Staff Miroslav Hlaváč at a command assembly of the Czech Armed Forces in Prague on November 24, and it comes after a series of Russian violations of Polish airspace.

The incident itself took place on Friday, November 20. Two missiles and a jet-powered drone came down in south-eastern Poland, in the Lublin and Subcarpathian regions, and Polish investigators later said debris from three Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles had been found. One missile landed roughly 60 kilometres from the Ukrainian border. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said the objects were Russian and described the incident as a deliberate provocation rather than an accident.

The Czech response was immediate. Prime Minister Andrej Babiš condemned the violation of Polish airspace as unacceptable and wrote on X that the Czech Republic stands firmly with its Polish friends and is ready to offer help and cooperation. President Petr Pavel said the same day that it was more a question of time before a similar incident also happened in the Czech Republic, and that Russia regards every country that supports Ukraine as a legitimate target.

At the November 24 command assembly, Zůna and Hlaváč gave the offer military substance. Zůna stressed that any involvement of the Czech armed forces would depend on the needs identified by Poland within NATO, and he referred to Czech AH-1Z Viper military helicopters, which are currently deployed in Poland. Hlaváč said the armed forces had already begun assessing possible forms of support over the weekend of November 21 and 22. "We have prepared scenarios for how we could help. I will present them to the minister, and we will wait for a Polish request and respond to it," he said.

Prague has not yet said which specific forces or equipment it could send to Poland, but the Czech military operates several air-defense systems, including older Kub systems and Swedish RBS-70 and RBS-70NG systems designed to counter threats at very short range. Czech troops already serve in forward-deployed forces in the Baltic region, and Czech helicopters help protect Polish airspace, so the offer builds on an existing contribution to NATO's eastern flank.

The November 20 incident was not an isolated one. Another incident followed on the evening of November 23, when a Russian unmanned aerial vehicle briefly entered Polish airspace, according to Polish officials. After the November 20 crash, NATO's commander in Europe, General Alexus Grynkewich, ordered the deployment of an AWACS airborne early warning and control aircraft to monitor Polish airspace. Poland's Foreign Ministry delivered a sternly worded note of protest to Russia's ambassador, and on November 26 Sweden summoned Russia's ambassador to Stockholm, citing the Polish airspace violation and a reported assassination plot in Denmark.

Czech leaders framed the incident as part of a wider pattern. Pavel said Russia is trying to divide European countries through hybrid action and to build the strongest possible position so that it can dictate terms to them, and that giving in to Russian pressure only invites more pressure. Hlaváč said Russia is continuing its war against Ukraine and wants to test NATO's cohesion, while its hybrid activity has spread to all European countries. At the same command assembly, Hlaváč warned that with defense spending at two percent of GDP the army is reaching its limits and its modernisation will slow down.

For now, Prague is waiting. The Czech government has also approved the country's participation in the second phase of the European SAFE program, which makes it possible to draw EU loans for military equipment. Poland, meanwhile, is testing its allies' readiness: Defense Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz said Warsaw wants Britain and France to confirm their military commitments after the violations. Whether the Czech offer turns into troops or equipment on Polish soil will depend on what Warsaw asks for, and on whether Moscow's probes of NATO airspace continue.
