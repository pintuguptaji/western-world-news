Israel seeks to jail Palestinian student activist without trial after dorm arrest

Mahdi Tounsi appeared with visible bruising after being interrogated by police on his association with Israeli-Palestinian organisations.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 23, 2026

Israeli professors, students and rights activists are calling for the immediate release of Mahdi Tounsi, a sociology student at Jerusalem's Hebrew University who has been held without charge for more than two weeks after he was seized from his dormitory room. Tounsi, 26, a Palestinian resident of East Jerusalem, faces a request by the Shin Bet and police to hold him for six months in administrative detention, detention without trial, based on secret evidence he and his lawyer have not been allowed to see.

Tounsi was arrested on the morning of Sunday November 8 at the Mount Scopus dormitories of the Hebrew University, where he studies sociology and criminology. His friends did not know where he was for more than a day. On Monday evening they contacted police to report him missing and learned he was being held at the Russian Compound police station in Jerusalem. His glasses were left behind in the dormitory. The university later said it had been notified of the planned arrest only minutes beforehand and that its representatives were not present.

Instead of bringing Tounsi before a court for a standard remand hearing, police submitted an order for his detention without trial, signed by outgoing Defense Minister Israel Katz. Administrative detention allows a person to be held on security grounds without a criminal charge or trial. Under Israeli law, the defense minister may issue an order for up to six months, subject to judicial review, and the order can be renewed. Hearings are held behind closed doors, and the court may consider material withheld from the detainee and his lawyer.

The grounds for the detention have been kept secret. Tounsi's family and his lawyer, Nasser Odeh, remain unaware of the specific allegations. Police have said he was arrested under a court-issued warrant on suspicion of a connection to a terrorist act, in an investigation conducted with the Shin Bet. Odeh has argued the authorities turned to administrative detention because there was no basis to continue holding Tounsi through the criminal process. 'If there is evidence, bring criminal charges,' Hebrew University professor Yael Berda told demonstrators. 'Everyone here who knows Mahdi knows there is nothing against him.'

Tounsi has told his lawyer he was questioned about his political activities, including his involvement with the Jewish-Arab movement Standing Together and other Israeli-Palestinian organisations. He is an activist from Silwan and has been active in organisations promoting Jewish-Arab partnership, including Standing Together and Ir Amim, both of which have called for his release. Friends say he had campaigned against administrative detention.

His treatment in custody is disputed. Tounsi appeared in court with visible bruising, and told his lawyer that officers beat him during his arrest, in the police car and at the station. When he appeared by video link later, his long hair and beard had been shaved off, and he was without his glasses, which he had not been allowed to retrieve. The judge ordered on November 19 that the glasses be returned. Police have said he resisted arrest and was injured when he fell from their vehicle.

The court process has already been delayed once. At the hearing on Thursday November 19, the Shin Bet, which was supposed to present the classified material behind the detention order, failed to send a representative. The hearing was postponed until Tuesday November 24, and Tounsi's detention was extended until then. The court did not approve the six-month order at that first hearing. At the second hearing, Judge Tamar Bazak-Rappaport heard the request again. Tounsi watched on a screen from Megiddo Prison, and the judge was expected to decide in the coming days.

The case has drawn support from inside the university. The Hebrew University's dean of students, Professor Zohar Kampf, came to the court on November 24 to support Tounsi, and lecturer Yael Berda asked to address the judge, although the judge declined. Kampf described his efforts to stay in contact with students across an increasingly polarised campus. The case has placed the university under pressure from students and academics demanding that it intervene.

Outside the court, about 200 students and activists gathered on November 24 demanding Tounsi's release, and hundreds of students at three Israeli universities had protested the day before. 'We're here to stand with Mahdi in front of the unjust system that is kidnapping students from their dorms without charges,' Aya Khateeb, a Hebrew University student and friend of Tounsi's, told Agence France-Presse. Eden Soussana, a philosophy student who lived with Tounsi before he moved to the dormitory, said the two had campaigned against administrative detention together.

Tounsi's case is unusual in one respect and typical in another. It is unusual because he is a student at a prominent university, with professors and a dean publicly supporting him, and because the Shin Bet's failure to send a representative left the state's case unpresented. It is typical because administrative detention is widely used: Israel was holding more than 3,100 people in administrative detention in October, according to Israel Prison Service figures published by the rights group HaMoked, more than twice the number before Hamas's attack on October 7, 2023. The order was signed by Katz, the same minister who ended administrative detention for Jewish settlers in November 2024. If it is approved, Tounsi would spend six months in detention without charge, effectively a whole academic year, and it could then be renewed.
