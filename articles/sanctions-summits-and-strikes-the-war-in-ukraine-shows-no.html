Sanctions, summits and strikes: The war in Ukraine shows no sign of slowing

Sanctions, summits and strikes continue side by side as the war in Ukraine shows no sign of slowing, Euronews reports.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 23, 2026

It was a week of frantic diplomacy, high-level summits and promises of peace. Yet Russia's war in Ukraine showed no sign of slowing. If anything, the fighting spread beyond Ukraine's borders, raising awkward questions about what all that diplomacy had actually achieved.

The week began with an unprecedented summit. On November 19, US President Donald Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping met in Shenzhen, in the first trilateral meeting of its kind. The three leaders issued a joint declaration on nuclear security, international institutions and energy cooperation. Notably, it contained no commitment to end Russia's war against Ukraine. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, meeting with the Coalition of the Willing in Berlin at the same time, rejected the prospect of a new Yalta-style settlement in which major powers decide the future of European countries without their participation.

Hours after the summit, Russia launched one of its largest combined attacks of the war. Overnight on November 20, Russian forces fired around 100 missiles and 400 drones, including cruise and ballistic missiles and jet-powered unmanned aircraft, at targets across Ukraine, according to the Ukrainian Air Force. Kyiv was among the principal targets, and ballistic missiles damaged three bridges spanning the River Dnipro, disrupting transport between the eastern and western parts of the capital. The scale and distribution of the assault suggested a coordinated operation against Ukraine's transport and energy networks.

The bombardment was, in part, retaliation. Ukrainian forces had struck the Kerch Bridge, the strategic crossing connecting Russia with occupied Crimea, three times within a week, damaging its road and railway sections and interrupting traffic. Russia's decision to target bridges in Kyiv appeared consistent with its declared intention to hit Ukrainian transport infrastructure in return. Putin also said on November 19 that Russia would no longer consider a moratorium on attacks on energy infrastructure or Black Sea shipping, and on November 24 he promised a response to Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russia had launched around 2,700 drones, nearly 1,460 aerial bombs and 165 missiles over the past week, striking 20 Ukrainian regions. Ukraine, meanwhile, appeared to be taking the fight further afield. A Russian warship was destroyed in a drone attack in the Libyan port of Benghazi on November 22, and a Russian cargo vessel was struck in the Gulf of Finland. Kyiv initially claimed neither attack, though Zelenskyy later confirmed the Benghazi strike.

NATO, too, was drawn into the week's events. Three Russian cruise missiles violated Polish airspace on Friday during multinational military exercises involving British troops. NATO ambassadors were due to meet in Brussels to discuss the incident, with Poland seeking additional support for its eastern border, and the alliance authorised extra airborne radar surveillance for Poland. Denmark, meanwhile, accused Russian intelligence of plotting to assassinate the Ukrainian-American rapper Kyivstoner near Copenhagen, prompting the expulsion of Moscow's defense attache.

Brussels answered with more sanctions. The European Commission was preparing to unveil its 22nd sanctions package against Russia, expected to target Russian banks, cryptocurrency platforms and the shadow fleet of tankers that carries Russian oil, along with companies in third countries helping Moscow obtain blacklisted goods. Having learnt from the previous package, which became mired in ugly vetoes, the Commission intended to keep this one neat and tidy, and to strike a deal before EU ambassadors left for Christmas.

Money was also on the agenda. EU leaders were preparing to discuss a 45 billion euro loan for Ukraine in 2027, split into up to 25 billion euros of military support and 20 billion euros of civilian support, though Bulgaria's president, Rumen Radev, backed only the civilian share. Zelenskyy said Ukraine needed 115 billion euros from outside sources in 2027, with about 90 billion already secured, and wanted more Patriot air defense interceptors. Estonia announced it would close its Russian border crossings from December 1 for six months.

Diplomacy continued alongside the fighting. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said a European team would join the next round of negotiations on ending the war. Jared Kushner, the US envoy, said Trump could consider NATO Article 5-style security guarantees for Ukraine if the risk of a renewed Russian invasion were sufficiently reduced. Germany's defense minister, Boris Pistorius, said refusing Putin's invitation to discuss European security would send an inappropriate signal, though he expected no near-term peace deal.

For all the summits and sanctions, the week's balance sheet was grim. European leaders were still discussing security guarantees, peacekeeping forces and negotiating formats. Moscow was still sending missiles. And Brussels was preparing to tighten the screws again. The war, in its fifth year, showed no sign of slowing, and the fighting was now reaching waters and airspace far beyond Ukraine itself.
