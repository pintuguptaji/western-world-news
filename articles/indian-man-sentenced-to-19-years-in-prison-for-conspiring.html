Indian man sentenced to 19 years in prison for conspiring to kill Sikh separatist in New York

Nikhil Gupta admitted to plotting with Indian government official to hire hitman to murder dual US-Canadian citizen.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 20, 2026

An Indian man was sentenced to more than 19 years in a US prison on Friday after admitting to orchestrating a failed plot, which prosecutors say was directed by an Indian government official, to kill a Sikh separatist leader in New York City, in a case that has strained relations between the United States and India.

Nikhil Gupta, 55, was sentenced by US District Judge Victor Marrero of the Southern District of New York to 235 months, or 19 years and seven months, after a hearing of about 80 minutes. He had pleaded guilty on February 13, 2026 to murder-for-hire, conspiracy to commit murder-for-hire, and conspiracy to commit money laundering. Prosecutors had sought 293 months, while the defense asked for 144 months, relying on a Probation Department recommendation. The judge imposed the low end of the guidelines range and ordered no supervised release, noting that Gupta would face deportation after serving his term.

The target of the plot was Gurpatwant Singh Pannun, a 56-year-old US citizen and attorney who founded Sikhs for Justice, a group promoting the Khalistan separatist movement, which seeks an independent Sikh homeland in the Indian state of Punjab. Pannun, who is general counsel of the organisation, was described by US prosecutors in their sentencing memorandum as an attorney and political activist. India has designated him a terrorist under its Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, a designation dating from July 2020.

According to prosecutors, the plot began in May 2023, when Vikash Yadav, described as an employee of the Government of India's Cabinet Secretariat, which houses the Research and Analysis Wing, recruited Gupta to arrange Pannun's assassination for 100,000 dollars, including a 15,000-dollar advance. Gupta contacted a person he believed to be a hitman, who was in fact an undercover officer with the US Drug Enforcement Administration. Prosecutors described the case as an attempted political assassination, aimed at killing an American citizen on US soil because of his political ideology.

The target was Gurpatwant Singh Pannun, a 56-year-old US citizen and attorney who founded Sikhs for Justice, which promotes the Khalistan separatist movement and an independent Sikh homeland in Punjab. US prosecutors described Pannun, the group's general counsel, as an attorney and political activist. India designated him a terrorist under its Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act in July 2020.

The sentencing drew a large crowd. Around 600 of Pannun's supporters attended, according to Sikhs for Justice, filling the courtroom and spillover rooms, many having traveled from across the United States and Canada. Kamaljeet Sidhu, a volunteer with the organisation, said the case was a matter of concern for all of them: Tomorrow, I may be the target. Reading a statement, Gupta expressed regret and quoted Mahatma Gandhi on nonviolence, prompting laughter from Sikh spectators. In a letter to the judge sent a week earlier, he said his judgement had been impaired by heavy drinking and personal struggles, and described how, in solitary confinement, he had turned to yoga, meditation and spiritual books.

Pannun began his victim statement with a passage from the Guru Granth Sahib, the central scripture of Sikhism, spoke of the thousands of Sikhs tortured and killed by the Indian government, and thanked the Department of Justice for defending his right to free speech, ending: may God bless America, the land of the brave and the home of the free. Outside court, he called Gupta a foot soldier and said his supporters sought accountability for the chain of command, calling for further investigation of the Indian government, including Prime Minister Narendra Modi. The Indian Embassy in Washington did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

The case cannot be separated from the killing of another Sikh separatist leader, Hardeep Singh Nijjar, shot dead outside his gurdwara in British Columbia on June 18, 2023. Canadian officials alleged the masked gunmen had credible links to the Indian government, fraying diplomatic ties, and four men are awaiting trial in Canada. India has denied involvement in both plots. Yadav, the alleged handler in the New York case, has been charged in the United States but is not in US custody, and India says he is no longer a government employee.

The dispute has a long history. The Khalistan movement, which advocates an independent Sikh state, was marked by violence from the 1970s to the 1990s, including the bombing of an Air India flight. Its roots lie in grievances including the 1984 military action in which government forces entered the Golden Temple, the holiest Sikh shrine, killing an estimated 3,000 civilians and pilgrims. Today, support for Khalistan inside Punjab is tepid, and the movement is sustained largely by diaspora activists, who say they pursue their campaign through a peaceful referendum. Pannun said after the sentencing that he was ready to face India's bullet if that was the price he had to pay.

Gupta's sentencing closes the criminal case against the man prosecutors called the organizer of the plot, but wider questions remain open. A separate civil action brought by Pannun continues in New York, where on November 13 a judge ordered summonses for India's National Security Adviser, Ajit Doval, and the former head of the Research and Analysis Wing, Samant Goel, under the Hague Convention. For the United States, the case has become a test of how it treats alleged foreign plots on its own soil. For India, it remains a diplomatic wound neither side has closed.
