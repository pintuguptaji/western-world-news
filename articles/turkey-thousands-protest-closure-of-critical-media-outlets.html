Turkey: Thousands protest closure of critical media outlets

Protesters gathered in Istanbul to show their support for press freedom in Turkey. The protest was called just days after the police raided Sozcu TV and arrested several senior staff members.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 22, 2026

WWNEurope

Several thousand people marched through Istanbul on Sunday, November 22, to protest against the takeover of one of Turkey’s last critical television channels, the latest in a series of state interventions that press freedom advocates say has left almost no independent media standing in the country.

The demonstrators gathered in Besiktas, on the European side of the city, and marched towards the Bosphorus carrying a banner that read “We want freedom of thought, freedom of the press and freedom for journalists.” The protest was called by the Yeni Parti, or New Party, a small opposition party, but it drew support from journalists, activists and ordinary citizens who said the closure of the channel was a step too far.

The channel at the center of the protest is Sözcü TV, the television arm of the Sözcü newspaper group, which has long been one of the most prominent critics of President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan’s government. In the early hours of Tuesday, November 17, police raided the channel’s Istanbul offices and the state savings deposit insurance fund, the TMSF, appointed a trustee to run it. The channel was briefly taken off the air. Its print edition was not affected by the takeover.

The official reason given for the intervention was an investigation into alleged links between the channel’s owner, Burak Akbay, and the Gülen movement, the religious network that the Turkish government blames for the failed coup attempt of 2016. Four people were detained in connection with the investigation: Funda Tuncer Sıdalı, the chair of the channel’s board, Mustafa Asım Akgül, its former general manager, Yonca Yücekaleli, its fiscal manager, and İsmail Öz. All four were released on November 20 under judicial supervision, with one of them placed under house arrest.

The protesters in Istanbul did not accept the official explanation. They said the takeover was part of a pattern: the government, they argued, uses the courts and the TMSF to silence media outlets it cannot control, and the Gülen allegation is a pretext. The evidence for that argument is the list of channels that have suffered the same fate. Sözcü TV is the third critical television channel to be taken over in recent weeks, after Halk TV on October 14 and Tele1, and it is not the only one. The independent news website T24 has been blocked since September 30, and at least 10 media outlets have been placed under TMSF trusteeship since March 2025.

The owners of the earlier channels deny the allegations against them. Cafer Mahiroğlu, the owner of Halk TV, has denied the accusations of bribery, bid-rigging and money laundering that were made against him, and has said the takeover was politically motivated. The government has denied that, saying the interventions are based on criminal investigations and have nothing to do with the channels’ editorial lines.

The scale of the pressure on Turkish media is stark. According to the Media and Law Studies Association, 25 journalists are currently in prison in Turkey, and the country ranks 163rd out of 180 in the World Press Freedom Index compiled by Reporters Without Borders. In the past month alone, several journalists have been arrested in connection with an investigation into an investment fund scandal, with two of them placed in pre-trial detention.

The international response has been strong. Six press freedom organisations, the International Press Institute, Amnesty International Turkey, Article 19, the Committee to Protect Journalists, the European Federation of Journalists and the South East Europe Media Organisation, sent a fact-finding mission to Turkey from 23 to November 25, and called for the trustees to be removed, for the 2022 disinformation law to be repealed, and for the prosecutions of journalists to end. Timur Soykan, a journalist at the independent outlet BirGün, said the takeover of Sözcü TV was “a blow to the public’s right to information” and that the government was “trying to silence the last voices that tell the truth.”

For the protesters in Besiktas, the march was about more than one channel. It was about whether Turkey will have any independent media left at all. The government says it is enforcing the law; the journalists say the law is being used to enforce silence. The crowd that marched to the Bosphorus on Sunday was making the same point in simpler terms: they want to hear the news from someone other than the government.

Sources: the Committee to Protect Journalists, the Manila Times, Turkish Minute and The Business Standard.
