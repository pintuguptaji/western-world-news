OpenAI countersues Apple for allegedly stealing trade secrets

Lawsuit also alleging pressure on hardware suppliers comes after Apple sued OpenAI for allegedly poaching employees.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 27, 2026

OpenAI sued Apple on Friday, accusing the iPhone maker of stealing its trade secrets and pressuring hardware suppliers not to work on its future devices, in counterclaims filed in federal court in Northern California. The filing escalates a legal fight that began in July, when Apple sued OpenAI, alleging it had poached employees to gain access to its technology.

In the filing, OpenAI accused Apple of “unlawful, tortious, and unfair business practices” and of trying to “compete unfairly by any means available”. It said Apple’s employment practices were designed to “trap” employees and stop them from working on a rival’s technology, and that the company was using its size and wealth to hold back competition in the emerging market for AI-powered devices.

At the center of OpenAI’s claims is a former employee whom Apple rehired after he spent six months working at OpenAI. OpenAI said that during that time the employee gained access to specific information about a new device, as well as its roadmap for future hardware products. “Given the timing, it now appears that stealing those documents may have been his reason for joining OpenAI in the first place,” OpenAI said in the filing. Apple has denied using the rehired employee to obtain OpenAI’s confidential information.

OpenAI also accused Apple of “coercing and threatening” suppliers working with the ChatGPT maker, in an effort to hamper its entry into the connected-devices market. The filing alleges that Apple has pressured vendors not to work on OpenAI’s devices. Apple has denied trying to suppress competition in AI device manufacturing.

Much of the countersuit concerns how Apple handles employees who leave. OpenAI claimed that Apple encourages staff to do work on their personal Apple devices and accounts, then asks departing employees to leave on the same day they announce they are joining a competitor, preventing an orderly separation of work and personal information. The complaint says this creates “trapdoors” that allow Apple “to accuse former employees of trade secret theft”.

OpenAI further alleged that Apple continues to access former employees’ personal devices and accounts after they leave the company, enabling it to “monitor and ensnare departing employees”. The filing said that 400 employees have left Apple for OpenAI so far, and that Apple wants to intimidate others from leaving for rival technology companies.

Apple pushed back strongly. A spokesperson said: “This case is about OpenAI employees deliberately stealing, accessing, and using Apple’s secret and confidential information regarding our unreleased technologies, processes, and products, not about inadvertent retention of Apple information.” Apple called the counterclaims “a transparent attempt to distract from the substance of Apple’s lawsuit, in which OpenAI has already been ordered to stop using Apple’s confidential information.”

Apple said it trains employees to keep work and personal files separate, including through a work-specific iCloud account, and does not pursue claims against former employees who accidentally retain files on personal iCloud accounts. It said it has strict policies in place to make sure new employees do not use confidential information from previous workplaces, and would work with OpenAI to remove any of OpenAI’s information found on its systems.

Apple’s original lawsuit, filed on July 10, named OpenAI, its hardware subsidiary io Products and two former Apple employees, Tang Tan, a former vice-president of product design, and Chang Liu, a former senior electrical engineer, alleging they misappropriated Apple’s trade secrets to benefit OpenAI’s move into consumer hardware. OpenAI has denied those allegations. In October, OpenAI pledged not to use the information Apple claims as trade secrets, after the judge said he was “highly likely” to order it not to use the material until trial. The case is scheduled to go to trial on July 19, 2027. On Friday, OpenAI also withdrew its motion to dismiss the case, without giving a reason.

The dispute reflects the high stakes of the race to build the next generation of personal gadgets for the AI era. OpenAI, which announced a project last year with Apple’s former design chief to build devices meant to bring smartphone users into the age of AI, said in its filing that its AI-enabled devices threaten to “outcompete Apple on what Apple views as its own turf”. It accused Apple of suing because it “has struggled to incorporate innovative AI capabilities into its previously popular products”. Apple, meanwhile, has been racing to catch up with its rivals on AI, including a major revamp of its Siri assistant, while Amazon, Meta, Google and a host of start-ups are also competing in the AI gadget race. Both companies have requested a jury trial.
