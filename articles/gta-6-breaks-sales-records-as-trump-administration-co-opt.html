GTA 6 breaks sales records as Trump administration co-opt game and get ridiculed online

The release of the video game Grand Theft Auto VI has already broken all records.. and led the Trump administration to hijack the cultural moment via AI-generated posts.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 23, 2026

Grand Theft Auto VI has broken every record in entertainment history, and the Trump administration has tried to ride the moment. The release of the video game on November 19 has already broken all sales records, and it led the White House and other government accounts to hijack the cultural moment with AI-generated posts, drawing ridicule online.

The numbers are extraordinary. Take-Two Interactive, the parent company of Rockstar Games, said GTA VI took in 3.79 billion dollars in worldwide retail sales on its first day on sale, November 19, a figure that covers worldwide sales through the end of launch day and includes 32.5 million copies that Take-Two says were pre-ordered before release even began. That is just under five times the roughly 800 million dollars its predecessor, GTA V, generated on its first day in 2013. It is also more than the 2.94 billion dollars earned by Avengers: Endgame across its entire worldwide theatrical run, still the highest-grossing film ever made. The analyst Rhys Elliott of Alinea Analytics put four-day revenue at just over 4 billion dollars on roughly 45 million copies sold by November 22.

The launch validates a bet that took 13 years and around 5,000 people to build. GTA VI is set in the fictional state of Leonida, and its credits run to roughly 10,500 people across 78 minutes. Take-Two chief executive Strauss Zelnick says generative AI had "zero part" in building the game, and former Take-Two chief executive Ben Feder has called it "the last pre-AI video game blockbuster." The game holds a 99 critic score on Metacritic, based on 153 reviews, with no mixed or negative reviews among them. Take-Two has reiterated its outlook of 8 billion to 8.2 billion dollars in net bookings for fiscal 2027.

The figures come with caveats. The 3.79 billion dollar number is Take-Two's own estimate and has not been independently audited, and it measures retail sales, what shoppers spent, rather than Take-Two's revenue. The company's own guidance for net bookings in the whole launch quarter was 3.35 to 3.5 billion dollars, so one day of shopper spending already exceeds the top of that guidance, but retail sales and net bookings are different metrics, and Take-Two's actual cut is smaller after retailers and platform fees take theirs.

None of that stopped the meme wave. Within hours of launch, the game's hyper-saturated, pink-tinted art style, its loading screens, its "WASTED" game-over card and its map of Leonida became free material for government and party social media accounts, assembled with AI image and video generators at close to zero cost per post. In the United States, Vice President JD Vance, the official White House account and several other government accounts posted AI-generated GTA-style images within the launch window. Meta chief executive Mark Zuckerberg posted an edited screenshot of an in-game prompt, a mission to "pick up groceries" with a pink heart and the line "Priscilla will appreciate this," captioned "Wait which version did I get?" Elon Musk replied with a laughing emoji.

The ridicule followed quickly. The game's iconography was not new to this administration: in March 2026, the White House account had already spliced real footage of US strikes on Iran with the GTA: San Andreas "WASTED" game-over screen, including footage of the US torpedoing Iran's IRIS Dena, in a video reported by NBC News at the time. Senator Tammy Duckworth called it out directly, saying war is not a video game, and noting that six Americans had died and thousands more were put at needless risk. White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt defended the campaign's reach rather than its content, and 404 Media's Matthew Gault catalogued the worst examples from governments around the world, from a 3.79 billion dollar launch day to a parliamentary apology in Scotland.

The launch week was not only about sales and memes. Alexa D'Agostino, one of three credited narrative writers on the game, deleted her X account on November 24, five days after launch, as her old posts went viral and a culture war erupted over Lucia, one of the game's two protagonists. A leaked Xbox build surfaced on November 20, and Take-Two has had 3,007 GitHub repositories taken down for a PC port built from the leaked build with AI coding agents.

GTA VI's debut is now the benchmark against which every future entertainment launch will be measured, and it proved that a game made entirely by human hands, over 13 years, can still dominate the culture. The meme cycle proved something else: that AI image generators have made it almost free for official accounts to join a cultural moment, and that the cost of doing it badly is now almost entirely reputational. The White House and JD Vance got the engagement they wanted, and the ridicule that came with it.
