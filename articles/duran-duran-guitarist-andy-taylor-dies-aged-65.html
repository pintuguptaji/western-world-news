Duran Duran guitarist Andy Taylor dies aged 65

Andy Taylor, whose guitar riffs helped shape Duran Duran's distinctive sound and propel the British band to global fame in the 1980s, has died aged 65 after a long battle with prostate cancer, the band and his family said Wednesday.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 25, 2026

Duran Duran guitarist Andy Taylor has died at the age of 65, the band announced on Wednesday, November 25, four years after revealing that he had been diagnosed with stage four prostate cancer.

Taylor died on Monday, November 23, at his home, surrounded by his family, according to a joint statement from the band and his family. He had been living with metastatic prostate cancer since 2018, a diagnosis he made public in 2022 when he was unable to attend the band’s induction into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame. The band’s singer, Simon Le Bon, read out a letter from Taylor at the ceremony in which he described his illness.

Born on February 16, 1961 in Tynemouth, in the north-east of England, Taylor was a founding member of Duran Duran and one of the five musicians who made up the band’s classic “Fab Five” line-up. His guitar playing, a mix of rock riffs and funk rhythms, was central to the band’s sound, and his work on hits such as “Rio,” “The Reflex” and “Hungry Like the Wolf” helped make Duran Duran one of the biggest bands of the 1980s. The band won two Grammy Awards and sold tens of millions of records around the world.

Taylor’s influence went beyond Duran Duran. He was a member of The Power Station, a supergroup formed in the mid-1980s with the band’s bassist John Taylor and two members of Chic, and he had a solo career that included several albums and collaborations. He was, by the band’s own account, the rock edge in a group otherwise defined by synthesisers and bass, the guitarist who gave their pop songs their bite.

His illness had been public knowledge since 2022, and Taylor had been open about the treatment he was receiving. In an interview in 2023, he said he had been classified as receiving palliative, end-of-life care, and that the treatment he had since received had given him his life back. “I was classified as palliative, end-of-life care,” he said. “And now I’m not, I’m asymptomatic.” He described staying alive as “a full-time job,” and spoke candidly about the toll the disease had taken on him and his family.

The band’s tribute was heartfelt. “There’s a massive Andy Taylor shaped hole in my heart,” Le Bon said in a statement. “Andy was the rock in our band, the one who kept us grounded when the rest of us were flying off in all directions. We loved him, and we will miss him more than words can say.” The band asked that donations be made to Prostate Cancer UK in his memory.

Taylor is survived by his wife, Tracey, to whom he had been married for 40 years, and by their four children. He had lived for many years in Ibiza, where he continued to write and record music even as his health declined.

His death comes at a difficult time for the band. Two weeks earlier, the drummer Roger Taylor had announced that he was stepping down because of tinnitus, the persistent ringing in the ears that had made it impossible for him to continue playing. The band is now without two of the five members who defined its classic line-up, and the tributes that have poured in from across the music world have reflected not just the loss of a guitarist, but the end of an era.

Taylor’s own story was not without its difficulties. In his memoir, he wrote about a six-year cocaine habit, and about the toll that fame had taken on him in the band’s early years. But he had been sober for many years by the time of his death, and those who knew him described him as a warm and generous man who had found a settled life in Ibiza with his family.

For fans of Duran Duran, the news is the loss of one of the musicians who made the band what it was. For the band itself, it is the loss of a friend and a founder. And for the millions of men who will be diagnosed with prostate cancer, his decision to speak publicly about his illness, and about the treatment that gave him four more years, will be remembered as a final act of generosity.

Sources: People, CNN, USA Today and BMG.
