Democrats win largest House majority since 1992 after flipping North Carolina seat

Democrats have now won at least 258 seats in the midterms compared to Republicans, who hold at least 173 seats.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 22, 2026

House Democrats have secured their best general election result in decades, winning at least 258 seats in the 2026 midterm elections, the largest Democratic majority in the House of Representatives since 1992, and a result that sets up divided government for the final two years of Donald Trump's presidency.

The milestone was reached on Sunday, November 22, when Democrat Chuck Hubbard defeated Republican Representative Virginia Foxx in North Carolina's 5th congressional district, giving Democrats their 258th seat in the 435-member House. Foxx, who had served 11 terms in Congress since first being elected in 2004 and in recent years chaired the powerful House Rules Committee, lost by only 330 votes after a recount, according to the Associated Press.

The win was symbolic of a larger political shift. Longtime Republican strongholds flipped to Democratic hands across the country, sometimes for the first time in decades, as voters delivered what US media described as a sweeping midterm rout and a crushing rebuke of President Trump and the Republican Party.

The scale of the gains is striking. Democrats have gained 44 House seats from where they started on Election Day, four more than the 40 they picked up in Trump's first midterm in 2018. In the outgoing Congress, Democrats hold 214 seats; Republicans will have at least 173 in the minority. Four House races have yet to be called, meaning Democrats may still expand their already sizeable majority.

The 258-seat tally is the biggest Democratic majority since Bill Clinton was first elected president in 1992, when the party also won 258 seats. It is just above the 257 seats Democrats held when Barack Obama was elected in 2008, yet still short of the Franklin D. Roosevelt-era high-water mark, when House Democratic majorities swelled past 300.

The House was not the only chamber to flip. In the 120th Congress, set to be sworn in on January 3, Democrats will also hold 56 seats in the US Senate, their largest majority in more than a decade. Taking control of both chambers gives the party significant power over legislation, although President Trump, a Republican, has already pledged to veto bills the party puts forward.

Experts say the Republican drubbing was driven by voter frustration with the cost of living, the US-Israeli war on Iran, corruption and immigration raids. It was also widely interpreted as a referendum on Trump's second term, which began in 2025: as of November, only 32 percent of Americans approved of the president's job performance, according to a poll by Reuters and the research firm Ipsos.

Democrats have framed their victory as a unique opportunity to shape the final two years of Trump's presidency. Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, a progressive who won re-election in New York's 14th congressional district, called the political shift a “mandate for change”, telling reporters at the US Capitol: “This is a historic moment for our country. This is one of the largest classes we have seen.”

As part of a 100-day agenda, Democrats are planning swift votes to reverse the Republicans' Medicaid cuts and restore the Affordable Care Act tax credits the Republicans allowed to expire, measures that would put the new majority's priorities to an immediate test against a presidential veto.

The result also carries a warning for both parties. A majority this large creates vast opportunities to govern, but also intense pressure to deliver on campaign promises. With the Senate in Democratic hands and a Republican in the White House, the next two years will be defined by negotiation, confrontation and the question of whether a historic majority can turn a midterm wave into lasting change.

Sources: Associated Press, Al Jazeera and Reuters/Ipsos polling.
