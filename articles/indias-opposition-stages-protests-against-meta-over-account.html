India’s opposition stages protests against Meta over account removals

Indian Youth Congress issues a seven-day 'ultimatum' to the US technology giant, urging it to lift all restrictions.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 21, 2026

WWNWorld

The youth wing of India’s main opposition Congress party has staged protests outside Meta’s offices in Hyderabad, Mumbai and Gurugram, demanding the restoration of Instagram accounts belonging to its leaders, and has given the US technology giant a seven-day ultimatum to lift what it calls restrictions on opposition voices, journalists and creators.

The Indian Youth Congress launched its “Digital Azaadi” campaign after Meta placed restrictions on the official social media accounts of the organisation and its leaders, including its national president, Uday Bhanu Chib, and its national in-charge, Manish Sharma. The party alleges that on November 19 Meta illegally withheld the Instagram accounts of the two men on orders from the ruling Bharatiya Janata Party. The youth wing said in a statement issued with protest videos: “If Meta wants to do business in India, it must respect the Indian Constitution, not surrender to arbitrary BJP government orders.”

The protests began on November 20, when Youth Congress workers entered Meta’s Hyderabad office to protest and raise slogans, while demonstrations also took place in Mumbai and Gurugram. From Delhi to Bengaluru, party workers staged protests holding images of Meta chief executive Mark Zuckerberg dressed in a Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh uniform. The Youth Congress described Meta as the BJP’s “son” during its protest.

Two accounts were restored after Friday’s protests, Manu Jain, who runs the group’s social media, told Reuters on Saturday, but he said thousands of other accounts belonging to journalists, creators and influencers critical of the government remain restricted. The youth wing has issued a seven-day ultimatum demanding the lifting of all “restrictions on opposition voices, journalists and creators”, and asking how many Indian accounts were withheld on government orders in the past six months, how many remain blocked, and what explains what it called the automated blocking of posts mentioning Rahul Gandhi or criticising Narendra Modi and the BJP.

The dispute has moved from the streets to the courtroom. The Congress party has approached the Delhi High Court against Meta and the central government, alleging curbs on opposition accounts and posts mentioning Rahul Gandhi. In a petition filed on November 25 by Manu Jain, through advocate Rishav Ranjan, the party accused Meta of acting as an “instrument for the suppression of lawful political expression”, alleging that the company uses the opposition leader’s name and photographs as keywords to automatically restrict political posts by Congress workers and office-bearers on Instagram and Facebook.

The petition challenges Rule 4(4) of the IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021, and seeks to have it struck down. It argues the automated system restricts content without informing the publisher, explaining why, or giving them a chance to be heard. The Congress flagged several posts that Meta marked as “unavailable in India” between September 29 and November 23, including a birthday greeting between party workers, a wedding invitation handed to Gandhi, a tribute to his father, a post about a 3,000-kilometre march, and a post about a lecture Gandhi delivered at the University of Chicago. The common feature across these posts, the petition says, was a photograph or mention of Rahul Gandhi.

The row has its roots in a change to India’s IT law made in February. Social media platforms must now remove content deemed unlawful by an authority within three hours rather than 36, and large platforms such as YouTube, X and Meta must deploy technical measures to proactively identify and remove copies of previously blocked content. The Indian Express reported nearly 195,000 blocking orders to Instagram, Facebook and YouTube between March and July.

The effect has been dramatic. Meta’s disclosures show it restricted 5.44 million items in India in the first half of 2026, about 130 times the 41,357 in the previous six months, and far more than the nearly 70,000 in all of 2025. Meta’s 5.44 million content restrictions in India were second only to Indonesia, where the number stood at 20.6 million. The Congress says the restrictions tilt the playing field against it ahead of state elections early next year, when Facebook and Instagram will be central to campaigning.

Meta officials met Congress social media chief Supriya Shrinate at a five-star hotel in Delhi on Tuesday, a day before the party went to court. According to sources, Meta representatives stated that the company was merely complying with Indian law and pointed to objections raised by the government. Shrinate argued that Meta’s actions discriminate against the opposition and interfere in India’s democracy, contrasting Meta with X, which she said allows users to explain themselves before posts or accounts face action. She also alleged bias over the removal of Congress’s AI-generated posts while BJP AI-generated content remained online.

The BJP has not commented on the lawsuit but has called the Congress protests vandalism. The Congress, meanwhile, faces criticism of its own: in Kerala, where it leads the state government, it has been accused of enforcing similar restrictions, with 15,627 pieces of content removed within six months of the government coming to power, according to critics cited by Deshabhimani. The campaign has drawn international attention, the Youth Congress said on November 27 that it had been covered by 106 media sources, but it has also exposed an uncomfortable fact: the politics of content takedowns in India cuts across party lines.
