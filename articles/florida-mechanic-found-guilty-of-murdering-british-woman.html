Florida mechanic found guilty of murdering British woman who attorney says came to US to die

Dwain Hall was convicted of stabbing Sonia Exelby, a music teacher who prosecutors say suffered from mental illness.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 20, 2026

A Florida mechanic has been found guilty of murdering a British music teacher he met online, after a trial that laid bare a two-year campaign of online grooming and a killing prosecutors described as premeditated. Dwain Hall, 54, was convicted by a Marion County jury on November 20 of premeditated first-degree murder and kidnapping, and was sentenced immediately afterwards to two consecutive life terms.

The victim, Sonia Exelby, was 32 and from Portsmouth, England, where she worked as a piano and music teacher. She was described by those who knew her as a talented musician “with a deep sensitivity and an enormous capacity for compassion and empathy”, and she had struggled with her mental health for much of her life, according to reporting on the case.

Hall and Exelby met on Motherless.com, a now-defunct fetish website, where Hall used the name “AlphaSadist”. According to the Fifth Judicial Circuit State Attorney’s Office, the two communicated for about two years, latterly through an encrypted platform called Sessions. Prosecutors said Hall groomed the vulnerable woman over that period, and that she eventually offered him $4,000 to kill her.

Exelby flew to Florida in October 2025 and was reported missing soon afterwards. Hampshire police in England began an investigation after an Interpol request, and found that she had flown to Gainesville three days earlier. Hall picked her up at Gainesville Regional Airport and drove her to an Airbnb. Investigators later established that he had bought items including rope and a shovel before she arrived.

Her body was found on October 17, 2025, buried in a shallow grave in a wooded area of Marion Oaks. An autopsy determined that she had suffered four sharp-force injuries, and her death was ruled a homicide. The case began on October 13, 2025, according to the State Attorney’s Office.

Detectives built the case from phone and online records, which established the two-year correspondence, and from physical evidence. A label from a shovel was found in the grave and matched a receipt for a purchase made by Hall, captured on a surveillance camera. The shovel itself was recovered from Hall’s home, and examiners found blood matching Exelby’s on its handle. Investigators also discovered that Hall had mailed a knife to a friend out of state; it was recovered and determined to be the murder weapon.

Hall initially denied knowing Exelby, but the investigation, involving the Florida Department of Law Enforcement, the Marion County Sheriff’s Office and authorities in the United Kingdom, tied him to her through the financial, phone and online trail. He was arrested and charged with murder and kidnapping.

At trial in Ocala, jurors deliberated for about an hour before returning a unanimous guilty verdict on both charges. The court heard that Exelby had traveled to meet Hall wanting him to kill her, but had changed her mind after arriving in Florida, according to the Daily Mail’s reporting of the trial. The court also heard that Hall assaulted her over two days before stabbing her to death, the same reporting said.

Judge Timothy McCourt passed sentence immediately after the verdict: two consecutive life terms. Hall’s wife, Ginger Stein, has continued to stand by him, telling the Daily Mail after the sentencing that he had done “the noble thing” by cutting short the life of the young teacher, a remark that has itself drawn attention in coverage of the case.

The case has raised difficult questions about online grooming, about the limits of consent, and about the platforms where such relationships form. Exelby’s death was the result of a plan made over two years of messages; the verdict, delivered just over a year after her body was found, means Hall will spend the rest of his life in prison.
