Freigang banned, Germany star to miss 2027 World Cup

Germany forward Laura Freigang has been suspended until October 2027 after missing three doping tests, ruling her out of next year's Women's World Cup in Brazil. Freigang denies any intention to evade controls.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 26, 2026

Germany forward Laura Freigang has been banned from football for 16 months for failing to keep her anti-doping whereabouts up to date, a ruling that will cost her a place at next year’s Women’s World Cup in Brazil, even though she has never failed a drug test.

The ban was announced on Thursday, November 26, in a settlement agreed between the World Anti-Doping Agency, Germany’s national anti-doping agency NADA, the German Football Association and Freigang herself. It covers three whereabouts failures, breaches of the rules that require athletes in the testing pool to keep the authorities informed of their location so that out-of-competition tests can be carried out, between March 11 20February 25 and 23 2026. Under Article 2.4 of the World Anti-Doping Code, three such failures in a 12-month period can lead to a ban of up to two years.

The suspension is backdated to June 8, 2026, the day the German Football Association notified her of the charge, which means Freigang will not be eligible to play again until October 8, 2027. She is allowed to resume training from August 8, 2027. The ruling also disqualifies all of her individual results, including her appearances, goals, assists, medals, honours, points and prizes, from February 23, 2026 onwards, although the results of her team are not affected.

Freigang, 28, has been a regular in the German national team for several years, with 45 caps and 17 goals, and plays her club football for Eintracht Frankfurt. The World Cup, which runs from June 24 to July 2027 in Brazil, was to be the centrepiece of her career. Instead, she will watch it from home, and with her Frankfurt contract running only to June 2027, her future at the club is also uncertain.

She has been open about how she got here. In an emotional video posted on Instagram, which she said she had recorded several times before sharing, she described the situation as heartbreaking and denied any intention of cheating. “I was never suspected of doping,” she said in the roughly four-minute clip, describing herself as “always a clean athlete.” She blamed inconsistencies and misunderstandings in the daily whereabouts updates she was required to file, and said she had made serious mistakes.

Her defense was backed by her federation and by her club. The German Football Association said it had supported Freigang throughout the process and stressed that the violations were “not based on positive tests or doping suspicion, but on formal violations.” Eintracht Frankfurt said she had never given the club any reason to doubt her integrity, and that discussions about her future were continuing even though her contract expires in June 2027.

The case raises uncomfortable questions about a system that most sports fans never see. Under the whereabouts rules, elite athletes in registered testing pools must file their location every day, including a daily 60-minute window during which they must be available for testing. A missed test, or a failure to update the information, counts as a strike, and three strikes in a year can end a career, even without any evidence of doping. Freigang’s case is not unique: similar bans have ended the seasons of athletes across several sports, and the rules have long been criticised as unforgiving for athletes who are not taking banned substances.

The timing adds a further sting. The rules themselves are about to change: from January 1, 2027, decisions in such cases in Germany will be made by a sports arbitration court rather than by the national anti-doping agency, as reported by the Süddeutsche Zeitung, but that change comes too late for Freigang, whose case was decided under the old arrangement.

For Germany, the loss is significant. The national team will go to Brazil without one of its most experienced forwards, and Freigang’s own career, 45 internationals, 17 goals, and a reputation built over years, now carries a 16-month gap that no amount of explanation will erase. Her case will be cited for years as an example of how the anti-doping system works: it caught no cheat, but it still cost a player her World Cup.

Sources: Associated Press, BBC Sport and CNA.
