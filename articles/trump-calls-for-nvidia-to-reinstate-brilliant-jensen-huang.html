Trump calls for Nvidia to reinstate ‘brilliant’ Jensen Huang

US president calls tech giant to give former CEO 'a second chance' and urges him to 'make a big apology'.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 21, 2026

WWNUS

Jensen Huang has been reinstated as chief executive of Nvidia, marking a stunning reversal just days after his ouster from one of the world’s most valuable companies shook global markets, and after President Donald Trump intervened directly to get him his job back. Nvidia’s board voted unanimously on Monday, November 23, to reinstate Huang as president and chief executive, in a decision the company said followed input from senior levels of the US government.

Huang’s fall had been swift. On October 29, the New York Times reported that he had had extramarital affairs with a senior executive who reported to him and with Alice Huang, a Megaspeed executive whom US officials suspected was a Chinese intelligence asset. He resigned on November 12 after growing pressure from Congress to investigate whether he had been targeted in a foreign intelligence operation. The board unanimously accepted his resignation and told investors he would not return. Nvidia’s shares fell roughly 30%, dragging down the broader market and fuelling fears of far-reaching contagion.

Trump, who had not heard of Huang when he began his second term, decided there was only one solution: getting Huang, a friend and ally, his job back. Over the weekend, Attorney General Todd Blanche presented an opening, informing Trump and Vice President JD Vance on Saturday that Huang was not the target or the subject of any Justice Department investigation, and that the department had no evidence to suggest he had broken laws or was a national security risk. That assessment rested in part on Nvidia’s months-long cooperation, under Huang, with Justice Department investigations into suspected smuggling of powerful AI chips by two of the company’s customers into China.

What followed were a frantic 48 hours in which the president, the vice-president and other top administration officials relayed Blanche’s assurances to board members as they rushed to rescue a leader many in Washington and Silicon Valley had dismissed as finished. Trump had initially suggested Nvidia hire former Apple chief executive Tim Cook, but changed his mind after seeing the market’s response and hearing from executives such as Elon Musk, who helped lead Silicon Valley’s support for Huang.

After a marathon board session on Sunday afternoon, Nvidia announced on Monday morning, before the market opened, that the comeback was complete. “The response from the market has been very positive,” Trump said in an interview with the Wall Street Journal on Monday evening. “That really shows the decision was right.” Nvidia’s stock notched the largest one-day market capitalisation gain on record for a US company, recovering much of its slide.

Trump took credit openly. “I spoke to them, let me put it that way,” he said in an interview with CNBC shortly after the announcement. “And I convinced them. I made the deal.” He said Nvidia’s board had asked for “assurance on the national security threat”, “and we gave them assurance, at least with everything we know”. The board said it was “confident that Mr Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk”, and the company formed a new committee to oversee its national security policies.

Huang himself struck a sombre tone a day after his reinstatement. At an all-hands meeting at the company’s Santa Clara headquarters on Tuesday, he apologised to employees, acknowledged violating Nvidia’s code of conduct, and said he had been in daily therapy to address the behavior that led to his brief ouster. He told staff he had let them down, called it the “hardest company meeting” he had ever done, and said he was grateful to have a second chance to lead the company he co-founded three decades ago. “Thank you all for the second chance,” he told employees in a video published by CNBC. “I’m going to be a better CEO the second time around.”

He insisted he was not reinstated because he was a “tech bro” above the law, and said the board would hold him to account going forward. He thanked chief financial officer Colette Kress for leading the company while he was gone, and said of Trump: “He is my president. I am in service of making our president, our country, successful.” He added that Nvidia was apolitical and would do the same for whoever the next president was.

The intervention has drawn objections in Washington from lawmakers in both parties. Republican Senator Dave McCormick warned in a Bloomberg Television interview that the president’s intervention could create a “slippery slope” in corporate governance. Democratic House Leader Hakeem Jeffries called Nvidia’s decision to reinstate Huang “indefensible, irresponsible and incomprehensible”. The behind-the-scenes lobbying was, as the Wall Street Journal put it, an unprecedented campaign by a US president to bend a company board to his will.

Trump’s personal involvement has been almost two years in the making. Huang donated $1 million to Trump’s inaugural fund and another $25 million to the president’s White House ballroom project, accompanied Trump to China on a state visit in May, and was seated with the president and China’s Xi Jinping at a state dinner in September. In July 2025, Nvidia reached a $4 trillion market capitalisation, the most valuable company in the world, and in October Trump bestowed the National Medal of Science on Huang. The question now is whether the comeback holds, and whether a president who can restore a chief executive might one day decide he can remove one, too.
