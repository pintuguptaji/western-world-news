Anthropic shares fall after blockbuster opening as wider tech sell-off continues

The AI firm’s shares dropped more than 12% on Friday, a day after a record $81bn debut that ended 17% higher.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 20, 2026

Shares in Anthropic fell more than 12% on Friday to about $85 by lunchtime, giving back much of the gain from a blockbuster stock market debut the day before.

The maker of the Claude chatbot began trading on Thursday at $111.50, peaked at $115 and closed up 17% at $97.17, after raising a record $81bn. The Nasdaq debut was seen as a crucial test for the industry and was largely judged a success, but the second day of trading was swept up in a broader tech sell-off.

By lunchtime on Friday, the S&P 500 was down more than 2% and the Dow Jones down 0.7%, with both touching correction territory, defined as 10% or more below a recent high. The Nasdaq Composite had fallen more than 3.4%. Nvidia and SpaceX were both down more than 6%, Tesla fell more than 3% and Meta was down 3.4%, while Apple, Amazon and Microsoft recorded smaller falls and Alphabet rose 1.2%.

The losses spread beyond the United States. In Europe, Germany’s Dax was down 2% and the FTSE 100 down 0.5%, while in Asia South Korea’s Kospi and Japan’s Nikkei 225 closed down about 5%. Taiwan’s market dropped about 6% on Friday, taking its weekly fall to more than 17%, its biggest weekly fall since 1990, with memory-chip makers including SK Hynix, Kioxia, Tokyo Electron and Samsung among the hardest hit.

Ed Moya, a strategist at market intelligence firm AlphaSense, said the debut had not changed the picture for AI stocks: demand remains very strong, but investors are asking how much spending is coming and whether it will deliver enough profit. Wall Street, he said, is starting to shift away from rewarding growth at any price. Analysts said volatility was to be expected in the first days of trading.
