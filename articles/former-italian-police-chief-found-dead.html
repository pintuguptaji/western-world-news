Former Italian police chief found dead

Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni has expressed shock following the death of a former national police chief, whose body was found on Saturday.

By Western World News Editorial Team · November 22, 2026

Gianni De Gennaro, the former head of Italy’s national police and one of the country’s most powerful security officials, was found dead at his country home in Tuscany on Saturday, November 21, aged 78, in what investigators believe was a suicide, nine days after his home was searched by prosecutors investigating one of Italy’s most notorious unsolved crimes.

De Gennaro was found at his home in the Tuscan countryside, where he had been living since retiring from public life. The district prosecutor’s office in Siena opened an inquiry into his death, and an autopsy was ordered. Initial findings suggested he had taken his own life, although the formal cause of death was not confirmed.

The death came at a moment of intense pressure. On November 12, prosecutors in Caltanissetta, in Sicily, had searched his home as part of an investigation into whether evidence was deliberately diverted in the inquiry into the murder of the anti-mafia judge Paolo Borsellino, who was killed along with five of his bodyguards in a car bomb in Palermo on July 19, 1992. De Gennaro was suspected of depistaggio, the Italian term for the deliberate misdirection of an investigation, and prosecutors had sought his house arrest. A judge rejected the request, finding the evidence insufficient, and the prosecution appealed.

De Gennaro had maintained his innocence throughout. In an eight-page letter dated November 20, the day before his death, which was published by the newspaper Corriere della Sera under the heading “My Moral Testament,” he wrote: “I never betrayed anyone.” He added: “A man condemned to death cannot lie,” and said he hoped the truth would prevail. The letter was found on a hard drive at his home that police had not located during their searches, and two more hard drives were seized from his son’s home and from a former bodyguard, along with two USB drives from a close associate.

De Gennaro’s career was long and distinguished. He had worked in the anti-mafia unit in the 1990s, alongside the judges Giovanni Falcone and Paolo Borsellino, and had been appointed chief of the national police in 2000, a post he held until 2007. He later served as head of Italy’s intelligence services, and as chairman of Finmeccanica, the defense and aerospace group now known as Leonardo. He was, by any measure, one of the most powerful figures in the Italian state.

The Borsellino case is one of the most painful in Italian history. Borsellino was killed two months after Falcone, who died in a car bomb in May 1992, and the two murders, ordered by the Sicilian Mafia, Cosa Nostra, marked the peak of the Mafia’s war against the state. The investigation into Borsellino’s murder has been plagued by allegations of misdirection and cover-up for more than three decades, and the question of whether evidence was deliberately diverted has never been resolved.

De Gennaro was scheduled to testify in the case next month, and his death has raised fears that a potential source of truth has been lost. Manfredi Borsellino, the judge’s son, said the death was a tragedy, and that his father’s case would now be harder to resolve. The prime minister, Giorgia Meloni, said she was deeply shaken by the news, and that the authorities would assist the prosecutors in establishing the truth. The justice minister, Carlo Nordio, criticised what he called a “trial by media,” and the deputy prime minister, Antonio Tajani, said justice should be decided in a courtroom, not in the press.

The funeral was due to take place in Rome on November 30, with President Sergio Mattarella expected to attend. For Italy, the death of De Gennaro is a loss that goes beyond the man himself. He was a symbol of the state’s fight against the Mafia, and his death, under investigation and under a cloud of suspicion, is a reminder of how much of that fight remains unfinished.

What happens next will depend on the investigation. The prosecutors in Caltanissetta will continue their inquiry, and the letter De Gennaro left behind will be examined as evidence. Whether it contains the truth, or only his version of it, is a question that may never be answered. What is certain is that the man who was once at the center of Italy’s security establishment died, at 78, as a suspect in the case that defined his career, and that the truth about the murder of Paolo Borsellino remains, more than three decades later, out of reach.

Sources: Le Temps, the Associated Press and Italian press reporting.
